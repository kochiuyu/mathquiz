# P1 length and distance (I): first screen tasks

**Source check:** 16 November 2026. The [EDB 2018 Key Stage 1 explanatory notes, 1M1](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=15) list four objectives: recognise length and distance; compare both intuitively; compare both directly; and compare both using improvised units. The notes distinguish straight distance from a route, require aligned endpoints for direct length comparison, and advise against ordering many objects at once.

## Released task forms

`lengthdirect1a` shows two labelled ribbons with starting ends aligned. Pupils choose the longer ribbon. Twelve variants alternate which ribbon is longer. The drawing does not disclose numbers; the key points to the farther endpoint. This samples visual direct comparison and the language of length.

`distancedirect1a` shows two straight lines from a shared Start point to A and B. Pupils choose the farther point. The key compares straight point-to-point distances, not travel routes. Twelve variants alternate the farther point.

`lengthunits1a` and `distanceunits1a` repeat those two comparisons with equal segments representing paperclip lengths. Each has twelve variants, 2–8 segments per object or distance, with a difference of at least two segments. Pupils choose A or B; the key counts both sets of equal units. The diagrams avoid centimetres and rulers, which belong to later units.

Each family has one-mark A/B scoring, a matching bilingual builder, pupil quiz, paper, worked key and three-question practice with a targeted hint, retry and first-try count. The task records are permanent and language-independent.

The KS1 curved-length requirement is explicitly addressed in B: compare two loose curved cords, straighten gently without stretching and align starting ends. A longer cord is initially coiled with a shorter endpoint gap, so pupils must compare the cord's length rather than its endpoint separation. Activities compare two objects at a time, not many-object ordering. Intuitive objects have visibly significant differences; distance always means straight point-to-point separation, not a route. Whole-unit teacher preparation avoids unnecessary fraction work. The extra different-unit reflection supports understanding but is not claimed as a fifth objective.
