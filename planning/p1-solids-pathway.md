20 November 2026. Eleventh student pathway; original English and Traditional Chinese teaching.

## Scope

The [EDB Key Stage 1 explanatory notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/en_ks1_e.pdf#page=20), printed page 14, place intuitive recognition of prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones and spheres in 1S1. The unit connects real models to pictures and includes touching, stacking, rolling and observation. This pathway supports that work; it cannot certify physical observations.

Examples are right solids viewed from different orientations, not oblique solids. There is no formal face/edge/vertex counting, naming of prism or pyramid subtypes, nets, volume or construction of solids. Those are not prerequisites for these P1 lessons. All teaching, questions and SVG drawings are original; the source supplies curriculum boundaries, not copied exercises.

## Learning sequence

| Lesson | Teaching | Easy → Normal → Difficult |
|---|---|---|
| Meet solid shapes / 認識立體 | Five broad families; curved and flat appearance; pointed shapes and whole-solid recognition. | Name an illustration; distinguish the pictured families; correct a tip-only guess and recognise rotated/recoloured models. |
| Shapes around us / 身邊的立體 | Match ordinary objects with models; focus on main shape; connect flat pictures to solids. | Match described objects; choose the relevant model family; disregard a sticker, distinguish two pointed models, and interpret a ball photograph. |
| Roll, stack and look / 滾動、堆疊和觀察 | Placement changes behaviour; a rolling model is not necessarily a sphere; predictions need checking. | Choose a supported observation; identify a useful placement; reject conclusions based only on rolling, a tip or colour. |

Each lesson has three teaching steps, two guided questions and three questions at each of Easy, Normal and Difficult. The **27 new practice items** bring the journey to **11 pathways, 33 lessons and 297 levelled practice items**. The six-question check samples two generated items per lesson. Retakes rotate three deterministic, overlapping configurations, not independent assessments. Practice can finish after correction; the existing 5/6 next-step recommendation is provisional, not a calibrated mastery threshold.

The third lesson asks pupils to interpret stated properties and plan observations. It does not pretend the browser observed a physical rolling or stacking experiment. Actual results depend on the object, placement and surface.

## Presentation and practical work

Original SVG illustrations use outlines and shading, with descriptive accessible text. Shapes appear in different orientations and two colours. Choices use visible numbers and bilingual words; colour never determines the answer. Pictures are schematic models rather than photographs of household objects. Hidden lines help show depth, not cuts in the models. Screen-reader descriptions necessarily provide an alternative to visual recognition; independent listening remains pending.

The collapsed real-model section links to the existing bilingual [P1 3-D shapes activity](../activities/p1-3d-shapes.html). It asks an adult to provide safe models, vary their size and colour, support gentle rolling and low stacking, and observe the pupil's work. The teacher key is initially hidden. No practical-completion badge is awarded from digital answers.

The time pathway now leads into this pathway. A successful final check links to the existing `solidname1a` Smart practice skill. Adult resources and their catalogue counts are unchanged. This is a teaching-pathway addition, not a new catalogue question type or a claim of complete P1 curriculum coverage.
