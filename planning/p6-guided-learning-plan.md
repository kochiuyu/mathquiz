# P6 guided learning plan

25 November 2026. **Planning complete; guided P6 lessons are not implemented.** This is a teaching sequence and objective map, with practical evidence and scope boundaries. Individual questions and detailed scripts will be written during implementation.

**43 numbered objectives in 15 content units, plus integrated 6F1 inquiry.** Follow the [P4–P6 sequence](p4-p6-guided-sequence.md). The next implementation remains **P4 multiplication by two digits**, followed by the remaining P4 lessons and review, then P5 and P6. Further general P1–P3 revision stays deferred.

## Prerequisites and open branches

- **Number:** decimal division → fraction/decimal conversion → percentage meaning → simple percentage applications. Non-integral equations use earlier algebra and the relevant fraction, decimal or percentage operations.
- **Circles:** P5 circle concepts → π and circumference → circle area with P5 area reasoning. P6 angle measurement is useful in shape work, but arbitrary-angle sector/arc formulae remain outside scope.
- **Volume:** P5 unit cubes and cuboids plus P3 capacity → unit relationships and displacement. Keep physical measurement evidence.
- **Time and graphs:** time conversions and speed comparisons can start independently. Teach line-graph reading before the travel-graph part of speed; neither route needs all percentage applications first.
- **Data:** averages can start after division and bar charts. Pie interpretation needs percentage/whole meaning and angle meaning for labelled-angle questions. Chart choice/critique comes after the chart families. Axial symmetry is an independent shape branch.

## Proposed lesson routes and objective map

All lesson labels below are provisional local references, not deployed IDs or fixed lesson durations. Split a long route into short sessions; do not force a pupil to read the whole sequence at once. Useful-first links are revisits, not score locks.

### 6N1 Divide decimals / 小數除法

Useful first: P4 division; P5 decimal multiplication, rounding and calculation order.

L1 Divide by 10, 100 and 1000 → L2 Divide by tenths and smaller units → L3 Whole divisors and decimal quotients → L4 Make the divisor whole → L5 Check columns and rounding → L6 Mix up to four quantities → L7 Solve and interpret leftovers.

[Unit audit](p6-decimals-iv.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-decimals-iv.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=50)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6N1.1 | Divide whole or decimal quantities by 10, 100 and 1000. | L1 |
| 6N1.2 | Divide whole or decimal quantities by 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001. | L2 |
| 6N1.3 | Perform the four required types of division involving decimals. | L3–L5 |
| 6N1.4 | Use conventional order in mixed operations of up to four quantities. | L6 |
| 6N1.5 | Solve decimal division problems, including contextual remainders. | L7 |

Practical evidence: Use place-value units and equivalent scaled divisions, write original columns, and separate a purchasable whole-item count from leftover money.

Scope: General divisions are bounded after scaling the divisor to a whole number: at most three dividend digits and two divisor digits, with the official exclusions 12.34÷5.6, 12.3÷5.67 and 123÷0.4. Special power-of-ten scaling is exempt. Mixed tasks retain applicable multiplication/division limits. Round to tenths/hundredths when required, use ≈ and do not round intermediates silently.

### 6N2 Fractions and decimals / 分數與小數

Useful first: 5N5 fraction-as-quotient meaning; 6N1 division and rounding.

L1 Decimals as fractions → L2 Fractions by expansion → L3 Fractions by division → L4 Exact or approximate? → L5 Compare without false ties.

[Unit audit](p6-decimals-v.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-decimals-v.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=52)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6N2.1 | Convert between decimal and fraction forms. | L1–L4 |
| 6N2.2 | Compare fractions through decimal conversion. | L5 |

Practical evidence: Represent the same amount in both forms and compare a close pair using enough information to distinguish it; explain why identical rounded displays need not mean equal values.

Scope: Decimal-to-fraction examples use denominators 10, 100 or 1000, then reduce. Round to tenths/hundredths when needed. Recurring-decimal notation and theory are not required. Keep exact rational values for comparisons and feedback; rounded approximations must not create false equalities.

### 6N3 Percent means out of 100 / 百分數

Useful first: 6N2 fraction–decimal conversion and identifying the whole.

L1 Name the whole → L2 Build a hundred grid → L3 Percent and decimal → L4 Percent and fraction → L5 More than a whole and small percentages.

[Unit audit](p6-percentages-i.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-percentages-i.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=53)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6N3.1 | Interpret percentages as a relationship between same-kind quantities. | L1–L2 |
| 6N3.2 | Convert between percentages and decimals. | L3 |
| 6N3.3 | Convert between percentages and fractions. | L4–L5 |

Practical evidence: Show 100% as one whole, compare representations using a stated reference quantity, and model a value above 100% without changing the whole.

Scope: Include percentages above 100 and fractional/decimal percentages with manageable arithmetic. Reduce fraction results. Always name the base quantity. Conversion practice alone does not teach percentage-change problems.

### 6N4 Use a percentage / 百分數應用

Useful first: 6N3 percentage meaning and conversions.

L1 What percentage is this part? → L2 Find a stated percentage → L3 Increase by a given percentage → L4 Decrease by a given percentage → L5 Estimate and explain the base.

[Unit audit](p6-percentages-ii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-percentages-ii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=54)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6N4.1 | Solve simple part/base and given-percentage increase/decrease problems. | L1–L5 |

Practical evidence: Build a bar showing the original whole and change, distinguish the change amount from the new total, and explain which quantity represents 100%.

Scope: Exclude discount, interest, complicated situations, percentage-relative-difference questions and finding the percentage rate of a change. Finding an original amount from a given percentage belongs to 6A1. A given 10% increase is in scope; asking what percentage increase takes 100 to 120 is not required here.

### 6A1 Equations with fractions and decimals / 分數和小數方程

Useful first: P5 equations; fraction/decimal operations; percentage meaning for percentage contexts.

L1 Keep both sides equal → L2 Use fractional and decimal coefficients → L3 Solve a bracketed group → L4 Combine like terms on one side → L5 Find an original amount → L6 Solve a shape problem → L7 Substitute and interpret.

[Unit audit](p6-simple-equations-ii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-simple-equations-ii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=55)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6A1.1 | Solve the specified equation forms with non-integral coefficients or constants. | L1–L4 |
| 6A1.2 | Form equations from situations, solve and check them. | L5–L7 |

Practical evidence: Model equality, retain each transformation, write a direct story relation and substitute the solution into the original context, including units.

Scope: Forms: ax+b=c, ax−b=c, a(x+b)=c, a(x−b)=c, dx+ex=c, dx−ex=c (d≠e). Here a,b,c may be whole, fraction, decimal or percentage values; d,e are whole numbers, and a,d,e are nonzero. Like terms stay on one side. Include original-amount and missing-length contexts when their shape concepts are known; no variable denominators, quadratic equations or unknowns on both sides.

### 6M1 Measure and draw angles / 量角與畫角

Useful first: P2 angle meaning, perpendiculars and right angles.

L1 A standard angle unit → L2 Read a protractor → L3 Choose the correct scale → L4 Measure reflex angles → L5 Name an angle → L6 Draw and check an angle.

[Unit audit](p6-angle-degree.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-angle-degree.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=56)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6M1.1 | Recognise degrees and standard angle references. | L1 |
| 6M1.2 | Measure and compare angles in degrees. | L2–L5 |
| 6M1.3 | Draw angles of stated sizes. | L6 |

Practical evidence: Fold reference angles, place a real protractor at the vertex and baseline, estimate after measurement experience, then draw and check a fresh angle.

Scope: Measure and draw strictly between 0° and 360°; recognise straight, reflex and round angles even though measuring/drawing 0° and 360° is not required. Use angle notation with the vertex in the middle of a three-letter name. Arm length does not determine angle size.

### 6M2 Volume and displaced water / 體積與排水

Useful first: P3 capacity and scale reading; P5 cubic units and cuboid volume.

L1 Link one cubic centimetre and one millilitre → L2 Relate litres and cubic units → L3 Read a before-and-after level → L4 Use a tank or measuring cup → L5 Use an overflow vessel → L6 Choose a suitable solid.

[Unit audit](p6-volume-ii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-volume-ii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=58)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6M2.1 | Relate capacity and volume units. | L1–L2 |
| 6M2.2 | Measure irregular-solid volume by displacement. | L3–L6 |

Practical evidence: Record original before/after readings, fully immerse a suitable solid, compare with overflow collection and explain the unit relationship.

Scope: Include 1 cm³=1 mL and 1 m³=1000 L. Distinguish internal capacity from external object volume. Use non-floating, non-dissolving solids, avoid trapped air/spills, and provide safe adult-supported apparatus. A supplied scale subtraction is not evidence of carrying out displacement.

### 6M3 Distance around circles / 圓周

Useful first: P5 circle radius/diameter; P4 boundary; decimal/fraction arithmetic.

L1 Measure around several circles → L2 Compare circumference and diameter → L3 Use pi → L4 Find a missing radius or diameter → L5 Trace semicircle and quarter-circle boundaries → L6 Combine straight and curved edges.

[Unit audit](p6-perimeter-ii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-perimeter-ii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=59)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6M3.1 | Recognise π through the circumference-to-diameter relationship. | L1–L3 |
| 6M3.2 | Use circumference rules in direct, inverse and composite problems. | L3–L6 |

Practical evidence: Measure several physical circles with string or rolling, retain imperfect measurements, and explain why a semicircle perimeter includes a straight diameter.

Scope: Use only 3.14 or 22/7 as specified approximations to π. Circles, semicircles, quarter-circles and composites are in scope; arbitrary-angle arc-length formulae are not. The usual decimal digit limits are not imposed here, but arithmetic stays manageable.

### 6M4 Time and speed / 時間與速率

Useful first: P3 time/length; P5–P6 fraction and decimal operations; line-graph reading before travel-graph lessons.

L1 Change a single time unit → L2 Find start, finish or duration → L3 Describe how fast → L4 Compare by watching → L5 Compare in a fair trial → L6 Use an informal rate → L7 Read m/s and km/h → L8 Tell a travel-graph story → L9 Solve distance, time and speed problems.

[Unit audit](p6-speed.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p6-speed.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=60)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6M4.1 | Convert between the specified time units. | L1 |
| 6M4.2 | Find an unknown start, finish or duration. | L2 |
| 6M4.3 | Describe speed as distance travelled per time. | L3 |
| 6M4.4 | Compare clearly different speeds intuitively. | L4 |
| 6M4.5 | Compare speeds directly in a fair trial. | L5 |
| 6M4.6 | Compare speeds using consistent informal units. | L6 |

| 6M4.8 | Interpret travel graphs. | L8 |
| 6M4.9 | Solve speed-related problems. | L9 |

Practical evidence: Run a safe model-car or walking trial, record measured distance/time and an unchanged comparison condition, then interpret a supplied travel record. Keep actual timings distinct from instant screen models.

Single-unit conversion is hour↔minute or minute↔second; intervals may combine hours/minutes. Do not require calculations mixing hours, minutes and seconds together, m/s↔km/h conversion or chasing problems. Graphs must state what distance is plotted.

## Inquiry within lessons

**6F1 is an integrated statement, not another numbered objective.** Use the [existing inquiry activity](../activities/p6-inquiry.html). Each branch should let a pupil choose a question or condition, save a prediction, try a method, record what happened, explain it and test a changed example. Keep original and revised thinking separate, with actual help recorded. Do not award quiz marks for self-reported investigation completion or claim suggested teaching hours were delivered.

Possible investigations: test when rounded conversions hide a difference; measure several circumference/diameter ratios; compare safe displacement methods; change a model car while keeping the trial fair; or redraw unchanged data with a different scale. A justified failed prediction is useful evidence. Do not invent observed timings or collect unnecessary pupil personal information.

## Delivery order

1. Decimal division (6N1), establishing the separate P6 entry; split scaling/division from mixed applications if needed.
2. Fraction–decimal conversion (6N2), then percentage meaning (6N3) and applications (6N4).
3. Equations (6A1), including direct story relations and substitution checks.
4. Angle measurement (6M1), volume/capacity (6M2), circumference (6M3), then circle area (6M5).
5. Averages (6D1) and line graphs (6D2). Line-graph reading is now available for travel graphs.
6. Time and speed (6M4), in the three releases specified in its route.
7. Symmetry (6S1), pie interpretation (6D3), then chart choice and critique (6D4).
8. Whole-P6 coverage and fresh application review, including cumulative P4–P6 connections and practical/inquiry evidence. Keep TSA-style practice separate from curriculum completion. After that review, return to broader revision informed by pupil use.

This is an authoring order. Shape, measure and data routes remain available when their prerequisites are known; completion of every earlier arithmetic topic is not required. Partial releases must name the objectives covered and leave the remaining objectives visibly pending.

## Assessment and implementation contract

Each topic needs concise teaching, controllable models where useful, guided examples, selectable practice demands, a small separate sample check and a linked practical task. Set task counts during implementation; this plan does not prescribe a large fixed reading or question load.

Checks must change the numerical situation or reasoning demand from visible teaching/practice, not merely change IDs or introductory labels. Audit content across worked examples, practice, check items and exposed activity keys. Include unlabelled-operation stories, explanation or model tasks where appropriate, and practical/oral evidence that screen marks cannot establish. Repeating a check is still a repeated sample.

Keep first attempts, supported retries, original predictions/estimates and later measurements separate. Store P6 guided records under explicit grade/topic/pupil namespaces, with independent Continue routes. Never infer lesson completion from a bank score. Preserve earlier grades and current bank/Smart-practice records, including stale tabs, scoped reset, malformed/future records and unavailable storage behaviour.

Use exact rational or scaled-integer arithmetic for fractions and decimals; distinguish equivalent value, lowest terms, requested form and requested precision. Numeric caps belong to each response type. Reject zero divisors, zero denominators and invalid numeric syntax. Algebra inputs need a bounded grammar, never arbitrary code evaluation. Accept an equivalent expression when appropriate, but require the original story relation when equation construction is being assessed.

Verify mathematics with independent calculations and representative boundary cases before browser checks. Then test both languages, required fields, keyboard focus, narrow/wide layouts, meaningful diagram descriptions, saved-work isolation and links. Keep data tables or non-colour cues available for charts. Practical apparatus, independent translation, screen-reader listening and pupil comprehension require separate review.

Before moving beyond P6, audit every objective against actual lessons and practical prompts, compare repeated contexts, and produce a separate fresh application review with selected practical stations and inquiry evidence. Do not label sampled success as full-year mastery or require a pass score for the next year.
