# P1 addition and subtraction (I): first released forms

**Source check:** 16 November 2026. The [EDB 2017 supplement, 1N4](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/pmc2017_e.pdf#page=14) and [2018 Key Stage 1 explanatory notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=13) specify two-digit addition including carrying, two-digit subtraction without borrowing, addition results below 100, and checking subtraction by addition. The same unit also includes three-addend addition, the grouping property of addition, column forms and simple word problems.

## Released task forms

`carryadd1a` has twelve permanent pairs of two-digit addends. Every ones-column sum is at least ten and every total is below 100. Pupils write the sum as a whole-number numeral. The worked step explicitly regroups ten ones as one ten, then adds the tens. A wrong answer that omits the carried ten is a **hypothesis** to probe, not an observed error.

`noborrow1a` has twelve permanent two-digit subtractions. In every pair, the minuend's units digit is at least the subtrahend's, so no borrowing is required. Pupils write the difference; the key subtracts units and tens separately and verifies with difference plus subtrahend. A wrong answer that reverses a units subtraction is likewise a hypothesis.

`threeadd1a` has twelve permanent three-addend sums. Each addend has at most two digits and every total is below 100. The prompt asks for left-to-right calculation; the key shows the first subtotal and then adds the third number. This assesses calculation, not a word problem or a pupil's choice of grouping. Skipping the intermediate subtotal is a design hypothesis, not an observed error.

`groupadd1a` has twelve permanent triples. Pupils add the first two numbers and the last two numbers separately, then write the shared total. The worked key shows that either subtotal plus the remaining addend gives the same result. No bracket notation or technical term is required; brackets are introduced later in 3N4. Every addend has at most two digits and every total is below 100. The task checks a written instance of grouping, not whether pupils spontaneously choose a faster grouping.

`columnadd1a` and `columnsub1a` each have twelve permanent two-digit pairs. Pupils see the operation in a tens-and-ones table and complete the two answer digits in aligned columns. Every addition requires carrying and totals less than 100; every subtraction needs no borrowing. The key displays the completed columns and explains the ones and tens steps, including optional mental carrying for addition and an addition check for subtraction. One mark requires both digits in the right columns. These tasks assess written column calculation and digit placement, not an independently drawn layout.

`storyadd1a` and `storysub1a` each have twelve permanent two-number word problems in four everyday contexts. Addition stories include joining and combining groups, with both carrying and no-carry pairs; every total is below 100. Subtraction stories involve taking away or leaving, use no borrowing and include an addition check in the key. The question asks only for the resulting count, with the unit printed beside one answer field. No three-number problem or written explanation is required. Choosing the operation from the context, rather than a displayed equation, is the assessed difference from the calculation families. The current stories do not test comparison-language subtraction or all possible problem structures.

All eight families have one-mark, exact-numeral online scoring, with full-width digits accepted and decimals, calculations, typed units and leading zeros rejected. The teacher builder, pupil quiz, paper, worked key and three-question practice share the same IDs. Practice has a targeted first-error hint, one retry, a worked step and a first-try count. The written prompt does **not** assess independently drawn layouts or spoken explanations.

**Still open in 1N4:** concrete examples showing when grouping speeds a calculation, plus an objective-by-objective review before any full-unit claim. Manual classroom, translation, physical printer and screen-reader checking remains deferred at the user's request.
