## Models and saved work

The equal-arm balance displays object-labelled pans and text descriptions. Lower pans correspond to heavier objects under a valid setup. Swapping objects moves the labels/pans while preserving the conclusion about the objects. An invalid empty-balance/stuck-pan example explicitly requires correction and repetition; pan positions alone are insufficient then. Static tests inspect actual drawn pan coordinates across both orientations, equality and invalid configurations.

Dial scales have ten equal intervals, endpoint/midpoint labels, a pointer and equivalent text description. Pointer geometry is checked independently against every interval. These are illustrations, not calibrated tools. Ten 100 g blocks give 1000 g = 1 kg; the model starts empty and restarts without saving marks. Size examples are labelled illustrative records, not invented pupil measurements.

Two practical notebook entries each require an already-measured reference, new target and original estimate before tool, actual result, reflection and help can be added. Estimates cannot be replaced after the event. Notes are self-reports; a human separately observes zero checks, placement and reading. Existing capacity and length notebook identifiers/behavior remain unchanged.

First answers, one supported retry, observations and original inquiry predictions remain separate for each topic and learner. The investigation asks whether the bigger object must be heavier, with fair balance plans and retained invalid trials. Damaged/future/oversized data are protected, stale writes refused and blocked storage labelled temporary. Models earn no marks. One duplicate capacity checkpoint in the plan was removed while retaining its content once.
