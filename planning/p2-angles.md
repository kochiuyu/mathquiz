## Source audit

Checked 16 November 2026: [EDB supplement PDF 37–39, printed 34–36](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/pmc2017_e.pdf#page=37); [KS1 notes PDF 42–43, printed 36–37](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=42).

Six objectives cover angle concepts; comparison; right angles/perpendicular lines; making/drawing perpendiculars; acute/obtuse angles; and making/drawing different angles. Comparison concerns opening, not drawn side length. Set squares identify right angles and perpendiculars. Perpendicular drawing is required through a point **on** the line and a point **off** it. The perpendicular connection is the shortest from an off-line point to a line and defines their distance.

No degree unit/protractor measurement, straight/full/reflex-angle categories or numerical angle calculations: those belong to 6M1. No formal proof or slanted-length calculation. Pointing to the common point and sides supports understanding; do not create an extra formal vertex-vocabulary mastery requirement not specified in this unit.

| Objective | Observable tasks |
|---|---|
| 1 · angle concept | Task 1 joins two paper strips, changes opening, points to shared point/sides, finds everyday angles and tests extending a side without changing opening. |
| 2 · comparison | Task 2 traces/rotates/overlays A/B/C with common point and one side aligned. A=B; C larger, despite unequal lengths and orientations. |
| 3 · right angles/perpendiculars | Task 3 checks real corners and diagrams with a set square; task 4 checks intersecting perpendicular creases and finds another real example. |
| 4 · draw AND make perpendiculars | Task 4 folds; task 5 draws through supplied on-line and off-line points, demonstrating ruler/set-square alignment and slide. Task 6 compares connections on both sides of the perpendicular foot, explores farther points, and identifies shortest perpendicular distance. |
| 5 · acute/obtuse | Task 3 uses one-right-angle reference: D right, E acute, F obtuse; obtuse remains smaller than two right angles. |
| 6 · draw AND make different sizes | Task 7 makes acute/right/obtuse models, draws each, checks categories and creates shorter-side/larger-angle counterexample. Task 8 explains all methods using retained evidence. |

Four pupil and two hidden teacher sheets per language. Real folding, tool handling, overlay, construction and object observations are mandatory evidence, not inferred from digital labels. Teacher records independent/prompted/not-yet/not-observed, actual actions, words and assistance. Diagrams scale responsively and when printed, so task 6 has no fixed centimetre answer. Finite sampled connections provide exploration, not a purported formal proof of a universal theorem.

Code-native diagrams avoid angle-number labels. Internal values only generate/test geometry. Static pupil diagrams vary arm lengths/orientations; no right-angle square mark reveals classification answers. Pupil accessible labels identify diagrams without giving categories; tactile/equivalent adult-supported tasks remain necessary where visual diagrams are inaccessible. No unsupported independent accessibility claim.
