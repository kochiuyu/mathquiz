# P2 Length and distance (III) · 2M1

**Full-unit resource status:** resource complete, verified 16 November 2026; all 4 numbered objectives and applicable remarks. Not pupil mastery or proof of delivered practical/inquiry time.

## Source audit

Checked 16 November 2026 against [EDB supplement, PDF 32, printed 29](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/pmc2017_e.pdf#page=32) and [KS1 explanatory notes, PDF 35–36, printed 29–30](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=35).

The four objectives are metre recognition; measurement/comparison of both object lengths and distances in metres; appropriate recording units; and estimation with ever-ready rulers. The notes establish 100 cm = 1 m, appropriate choice and use of metre rulers/tapes/trundle wheels, and personal references such as arm span, arm length and pace. Measurement experience precedes estimation; pupils estimate before measuring new targets.

Compound metres/centimetres may be converted to centimetres. **Single-unit to compound-unit conversion is not required.** Decimal measurement records belong to 4N7, not this release. No millimetres/kilometres, perimeter, decimal conversion, arbitrary prescribed body lengths or fixed estimation-error mastery thresholds are introduced. Choosing an available appropriate tool matters more than naming one supposedly unique correct tool.

| Objective / remark | Actual pupil evidence |
|---|---|
| 1 · metre and need for a larger unit | Task 1 builds a strip from ten consecutive 10 cm lengths, compares it to a standard metre and explains usefulness for a long room. |
| 2 · lengths AND distances; tool skills and comparison | Task 2 measures two ribbons and two floor-point distances, chooses tools with reasons, demonstrates zero/alignment/straightness/endpoint placement, records and compares each pair. |
| 3 · appropriate units and permitted conversion | Tasks 2–3 record real results in m/cm, then convert mixed units to cm, including an actual measured example. Fixed answers: 110, 205, 340, 400 cm. No reverse conversion. |
| 4 · ever-ready rulers after measurement experience | Task 4 measures a personal reference twice and chooses an approximate calibrated value. Task 5 uses that reference to estimate a NEW object's length and NEW distance before measuring, preserves original estimates, explains differences and proposes improvement. |

Tasks need real rulers, tape, strips, ribbons and safe floor markers. A metric trundle wheel is useful when available; not every pupil needs every tool. A real choice among suitable available tools is required. Prepare at least one target with a nonzero centimetre remainder, for example a ribbon exactly 1 m 10 cm, to guarantee an actual mixed-unit record for task 3. Sheets intentionally contain no fake “physical measurements” from responsive screen drawings. Teacher observes actions and reasoning, not just copied answers. Rubric records independent/prompted/not-yet/not-observed with actual evidence; oral explanation and transcription are distinguished. Mobility accommodations may use an arm reference instead of pacing. Accessibility assistance must not be misreported as independent tool use.
