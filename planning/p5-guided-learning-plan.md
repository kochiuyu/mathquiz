# P5 guided learning plan

25 November 2026. **Planning complete; guided P5 lessons are not implemented.** This is a teaching sequence and objective map, with practical evidence and scope boundaries. Individual questions and detailed scripts will be written during implementation.

**43 numbered objectives in 12 content units, plus integrated 5F1 inquiry.** Follow the [P4–P6 sequence](p4-p6-guided-sequence.md). The next implementation remains **P4 multiplication by two digits**, followed by the remaining P4 lessons and review, then P5 and P6. Further general P1–P3 revision stays deferred.

## Pupil experience

Use the shared year, language and pupil controls and **Learn → Try together → Practise**. Follow the [short pupil-copy guidance](learner-wording.md): show the immediate action, model or question, then the answer control. Teach one idea at a time. Keep required conditions and units visible, hints on demand, and adult interpretation separate. Do not turn this adult planning document into a pupil introduction.

## Prerequisites and open branches

- **Numbers and data:** P4 bar-chart rounding → large-number rounding and estimation → compound bar charts. Estimation methods need actual sample/group choices, not only rounded exact totals.
- **Fractions:** P4 equal wholes, reduction and common multiples → unlike addition/subtraction; whole-number multiplication and fraction meaning → fraction multiplication → decimal multiplication and fraction division. Unlike-fraction addition is needed before mixed fraction expressions, not before every multiplication model.
- **Algebra:** begin letters and expressions once quantity relationships are understood; it need not wait for all fraction division. Equations follow expressions and inverse operations.
- **Shapes and measures:** circles precede the solid-shape route. Teach cube/cuboid recognition before volume formulae. P4 rectangle area, perpendiculars and dissection support polygon area; no new solids completion gate is needed for area.
- **Later links:** polygon heights feed P6 shape equations; circles feed circumference/area; volume feeds displacement; compound charts feed line graphs and chart choice. P5 need not teach those later formulae early.

## Proposed lesson routes and objective map

All lesson labels below are provisional local references, not deployed IDs or fixed lesson durations. Split a long route into short sessions; do not force a pupil to read the whole sequence at once. Useful-first links are revisits, not score locks.

### 5N1 Large numbers / 大數

Useful first: P3 five-digit place value; P4 divisibility and rounding in bar charts.

L1 Build the next place → L2 Read and write zeros → L3 Count and compare → L4 Round large numbers → L5 Estimate a large collection → L6 Compare estimation methods.

[Unit audit](p5-multi-digit-numbers.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-multi-digit-numbers.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=30)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5N1.1 | Read, write and count large numbers; explain place values and parity. | L1–L3 |
| 5N1.2 | Compare numbers using the highest differing place. | L3 |
| 5N1.3 | Round to a requested large place and explain the neighbouring values. | L4 |
| 5N1.4 | Estimate a large quantity and discuss the accuracy of the method. | L5–L6 |

Practical evidence: Build ten-for-one exchanges, read unseen number cards, and estimate a large collection by grouping or sampling. Keep the original estimate, sample choice and later count separate.

Scope: Extend place value through hundred millions. Round to thousands, ten thousands, hundred thousands, millions, ten millions and hundred millions. A nine-digit input cap would be an implementation choice, not an official maximum; rounding may cross into a tenth digit. Estimating an unknown collection is different from rounding a supplied exact number.

### 5N2 Unlike fractions / 異分母分數

Useful first: P4 equivalent fractions, common multiples and same-denominator operations.

L1 Make equal-sized parts → L2 Compare and order → L3 Add unlike fractions → L4 Regroup to subtract → L5 Use three quantities → L6 Solve and estimate.

[Unit audit](p5-fractions-iii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-fractions-iii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=31)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5N2.1 | Compare up to three fractions with different denominators, including whole numbers. | L1–L2 |
| 5N2.2 | Add or subtract up to three unlike-denominator fractions. | L3–L5 |
| 5N2.3 | Mix addition and subtraction of three unlike-denominator fractions. | L5 |
| 5N2.4 | Model and solve fraction situations. | L6 |

Practical evidence: Partition equal-length strips, show two valid common denominators, regroup a whole before subtracting, and explain an independently chosen story expression.

Scope: Whole and mixed quantities are included. When comparing or calculating with three fractions, given denominators are at most 12; do not turn this into a blanket rule for every later fraction unit. A common denominator need not be the least. A three-number L.C.M. algorithm is not required. Accept lowest-term improper or mixed results and retain estimates before exact work.

### 5N3 Multiply fractions / 分數乘法

Useful first: P4 fraction forms and reduction; whole-number multiplication meaning.

L1 Equal groups of fractions → L2 A fraction of a fraction → L3 Regroup and simplify → L4 Multiply three quantities → L5 Solve and check size.

[Unit audit](p5-fractions-iv.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-fractions-iv.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=33)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5N3.1 | Multiply at most three quantities, including fractional and whole factors. | L1–L4 |
| 5N3.2 | Solve fraction multiplication problems. | L5 |

Practical evidence: Fold a common whole in two directions, explain the overlap, and compare products when a positive factor is below, equal to or above one.

Scope: With three factors, use at most one mixed number. Avoid cumbersome arithmetic and require reduced answers. Do not import the 5N2 denominator ceiling. Fraction division comes later; do not teach that multiplication always makes an amount larger.

### 5N4 Multiply decimals / 小數乘法

Useful first: P4 decimal meaning and multiplication columns; fraction multiplication.

L1 Scale by 10, 100 and 1000 → L2 Scale by tenths and smaller units → L3 Multiply a decimal and a whole → L4 Multiply two decimals → L5 Round and use approximately equal → L6 Solve and estimate.

[Unit audit](p5-decimals-iii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-decimals-iii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=34)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5N4.1 | Multiply a whole number or decimal by 10, 100 or 1000. | L1 |
| 5N4.2 | Multiply a whole number or decimal by 0.1, 0.01 or 0.001. | L2 |
| 5N4.3 | Multiply two quantities involving decimals and locate the product correctly. | L3–L5 |
| 5N4.4 | Solve decimal multiplication situations. | L6 |

Practical evidence: Track the value of a digit in a place-value chart, explain a product with an area model, and record an estimate before calculating a measurement or price.

Scope: General products use one- or two-place decimal operands and a digit burden no greater than three digits by two digits. The special scaling objectives are exceptions. Exclude 0.124 × 3.9, 12.4 × 3.42 and 12.41 × 2.6 from required general multiplication. Round to tenths/hundredths when needed and use ≈. Equivalent values such as 1.2 and 1.20 need a separate explicit written-precision condition before being distinguished.

### 5N5 Divide fractions / 分數除法

Useful first: Fraction multiplication; P4 division and calculation order; unlike-fraction addition for mixed work.

L1 Fractions as division and comparison → L2 Share a fraction → L3 Count fractional groups → L4 Explain the reciprocal method → L5 Divide three quantities → L6 Mix operations with three quantities → L7 Solve with leftovers and unit prices.

[Unit audit](p5-fractions-v.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-fractions-v.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=36)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5N5.1 | Interpret fractions as quotients and comparisons of two whole-number quantities. | L1 |
| 5N5.2 | Divide at most three quantities, including whole numbers. | L2–L5 |
| 5N5.3 | Use conventional order in mixed operations of three quantities. | L6 |
| 5N5.4 | Solve division and unitary problems, including contextual leftovers. | L7 |

Practical evidence: Use strips or measured lengths to show how many fractional groups fit. Explain why scaling both quantities preserves the quotient; keep whole-item purchases and leftover money distinct.

Scope: Three-fraction division and three-fraction multiplication/division contain at most one mixed number. No required ratio-colon notation. Finding an original amount from a given fraction belongs in equations (5A2/6A1), not this route. Fractional relative difference and fractional increase/decrease problems are excluded. General mixed expressions contain three quantities, not an arbitrary long chain.

### 5A1 Letters for numbers / 用字母表示數

Useful first: Whole-number operations and quantity relationships; fraction notation for division.

L1 A letter stands for a number → L2 Write a quantity → L3 Read multiplication and division notation → L4 Describe a relationship.

[Unit audit](p5-elementary-algebra.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-elementary-algebra.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=38)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5A1.1 | Use a letter to represent an unknown number. | L1 |
| 5A1.2 | Represent verbal operations and quantity relationships with expressions. | L2–L4 |

Practical evidence: Label a hidden collection, describe another quantity in terms of it, and test the expression with two possible values without treating the letter as an object label.

Scope: Use one unknown quantity. Connect 3x to repeated addition and x/3 to division. Do not require two-variable expressions, powers or formal symbolic manipulation beyond this scope.

### 5A2 Balance and equations / 平衡與方程

Useful first: 5A1 expressions and inverse operations.

L1 Both sides are equal → L2 Undo addition or subtraction → L3 Undo multiplication or division → L4 Solve two-step equations → L5 Write an equation from a story → L6 Substitute and check.

[Unit audit](p5-simple-equations-i.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-simple-equations-i.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=39)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5A2.1 | Explain an equation as equality between two expressions. | L1 |
| 5A2.2 | Solve the eight specified equation forms using balanced operations. | L2–L4 |
| 5A2.3 | Form and solve equations directly from situations and check the answer. | L5–L6 |

Practical evidence: Use a balance or drawn equal groups, record the same operation on both sides, and substitute the answer into the original relationship.

Scope: Forms: x+b=c, x−b=c, ax=b, x/a=b, ax+b=c, ax−b=c, x/a+b=c, x/a−b=c; a,b,c are whole numbers and a is nonzero. Do not label whole-number solutions as a curriculum restriction. A story equation must represent the stated relation rather than only x=an evaluated answer. Non-integral coefficients/constants and bracketed forms are developed in P6.

### 5M1 Area from cutting and rearranging / 剪拼求面積

Useful first: P4 rectangle area and dissection; perpendicular lines; fraction multiplication.

L1 Choose a base and its height → L2 Rearrange a parallelogram → L3 Pair triangles → L4 Pair trapeziums → L5 Split a polygon → L6 Compare two area methods.

[Unit audit](p5-area-ii.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-area-ii.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=41)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5M1.1 | Identify a corresponding perpendicular height for triangles and quadrilaterals. | L1 |
| 5M1.2 | Derive and use parallelogram, triangle and trapezium area formulae. | L2–L4 |
| 5M1.3 | Find polygon areas by decomposition or completion. | L5–L6 |

Practical evidence: Draw perpendicular heights, including external heights, cut and rearrange real paper shapes, and explain why coverage is preserved.

Scope: Height refers to the selected base; a sloping side is not automatically the height. Use convex quadrilaterals without requiring that term. Missing triangle/trapezium side lengths from area belong in 6A1. Composite problems may exceed five numbers but should remain manageable.

Useful first: P4 arrays/area; cube and cuboid recognition from 5S2 before formula work.

L1 Space occupied → L2 Compare obvious volumes → L3 Build cubic centimetres → L4 Count hidden cubes and layers → L5 Make a cubic-metre reference → L6 Derive cube and cuboid rules → L7 Combine simple solids.

[Unit audit](p5-volume-i.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-volume-i.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=43)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5M2.1 | Describe volume as occupied space. | L1 |
| 5M2.2 | Compare clearly different volumes by observation. | L2 |
| 5M2.3 | Recognise a cubic-centimetre unit. | L3 |
| 5M2.4 | Measure and compare volumes in cubic centimetres. | L3–L4 |

Scope: Do not confuse occupied volume with container capacity. No cm³–m³ conversion or cube-root recovery of a cube edge from volume is required. Formal capacity/displacement relations are in P6. Introduce cube/cuboid shape concepts before using their formulae; full nets work need not gate all early volume activities.

### 5S1 Circles / 圓

Useful first: P4 length and plane shapes.

L1 Find the centre → L2 Radius, diameter and boundary → L3 Compare centre-to-circle distances → L4 Draw a circle of a stated size.

[Unit audit](p5-circles.md) · [Practical activity](../activities/p5-circles.html) · [EDB notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=45)

| Objective | Planned teaching and response | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5S1.1 | Recognise a circle and its centre, radius, diameter, circumference and basic properties. | L1–L3 |
| 5S1.2 | Draw circles, including with compasses, from a stated radius or diameter. | L4 |

## Inquiry within lessons

**5F1 is an integrated statement, not another numbered objective.** Use the [existing inquiry activity](../activities/p5-inquiry.html). Each branch should let a pupil choose a question or condition, save a prediction, try a method, record what happened, explain it and test a changed example. Keep original and revised thinking separate, with actual help recorded. Do not award quiz marks for self-reported investigation completion or claim suggested teaching hours were delivered.

Possible investigations: compare two large-collection estimation methods; find different valid common denominators; rearrange a polygon in two ways; decide which net will close; or choose a compound-chart scale. Retain raw measurements and sources for real data. These are proposed investigations, not records of completed classroom work.

## Delivery order

1. Large numbers and estimation (5N1), establishing the separate P5 entry.
2. Unlike fractions (5N2), then fraction multiplication (5N3).
3. Decimal multiplication (5N4), then fraction division and mixed applications (5N5).
4. Letters and expressions (5A1), then equations (5A2); these may be brought forward when prerequisites are secure.
5. Circles (5S1), then solids (5S2); separate cross-sections/frameworks from nets/spheres if sessions become crowded.
6. Polygon area (5M1), then volume (5M2), with the cube/cuboid prerequisite already taught.
7. Compound bar charts (5D1), with large-number scales and original data.
8. Whole-P5 coverage, fresh application and practical/inquiry review; then start the planned P6 entry.

This is an authoring order. Shape, measure and data routes remain available when their prerequisites are known; completion of every earlier arithmetic topic is not required. Partial releases must name the objectives covered and leave the remaining objectives visibly pending.

## Assessment and implementation contract

Each topic needs concise teaching, controllable models where useful, guided examples, selectable practice demands, a small separate sample check and a linked practical task. Set task counts during implementation; this plan does not prescribe a large fixed reading or question load.

Checks must change the numerical situation or reasoning demand from visible teaching/practice, not merely change IDs or introductory labels. Audit content across worked examples, practice, check items and exposed activity keys. Include unlabelled-operation stories, explanation or model tasks where appropriate, and practical/oral evidence that screen marks cannot establish. Repeating a check is still a repeated sample.

Keep first attempts, supported retries, original predictions/estimates and later measurements separate. Store P5 guided records under explicit grade/topic/pupil namespaces, with independent Continue routes. Never infer lesson completion from a bank score. Preserve earlier grades and current bank/Smart-practice records, including stale tabs, scoped reset, malformed/future records and unavailable storage behaviour.

Use exact rational or scaled-integer arithmetic for fractions and decimals; distinguish equivalent value, lowest terms, requested form and requested precision. Numeric caps belong to each response type. Reject zero divisors, zero denominators and invalid numeric syntax. Algebra inputs need a bounded grammar, never arbitrary code evaluation. Accept an equivalent expression when appropriate, but require the original story relation when equation construction is being assessed.

Verify mathematics with independent calculations and representative boundary cases before browser checks. Then test both languages, required fields, keyboard focus, narrow/wide layouts, meaningful diagram descriptions, saved-work isolation and links. Keep data tables or non-colour cues available for charts. Practical apparatus, independent translation, screen-reader listening and pupil comprehension require separate review.

Before moving beyond P5, audit every objective against actual lessons and practical prompts, compare repeated contexts, and produce a separate fresh application review with selected practical stations and inquiry evidence. Do not label sampled success as full-year mastery or require a pass score for the next year.
