# P5 purchase stories to equations · family brief

Status: live as `purchasestory5a` from 16 November 2026. Manual pupil, language, physical printer and hands-on screen-reader checks are deferred at the user's request.

## Curriculum and scope

[EDB Key Stage 2 explanatory notes, 5A2 Simple equations (I), objective 3](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS2_e.pdf#page=39) require pupils to solve problems using equations. The notes show a money context and say to form the equation directly from the situation, rather than starting with a solved expression such as `y = 9 − 5`. Objective 2 includes `x + b = c` and `x − b = c` with whole-number constants. This family asks pupils only to **form** the equation; solving it is a separate task in `equation5a`. It is one project interpretation of 5A2, not EDB endorsement or complete coverage.

Prerequisites: understanding that `x` stands for one unknown amount, addition/subtraction of whole-dollar amounts, and equality. There are twelve permanent, one-mark variants. Six give a notebook's unknown price, a known pen price and a total, requiring `x + b = c`. Six give the unknown amount before buying a book, its cost and the amount left, requiring `x − b = c`. Amounts are positive whole dollars; the hidden value is positive. The contexts never require change, discounts, decimal money, several unknowns or two-step equations.

## Bilingual item contract

| Element | English | 繁體中文 |
|---|---|---|
| Example | A notebook and a pen cost $19 altogether. The pen costs $7. Let x be the notebook's price in dollars. Write an equation using x. Do not solve it. | 一本筆記簿和一枝筆共售19元，筆售7元。設 x 是筆記簿的價錢（元）。用 x 列出方程，不用求解。 |
| One-mark answer | `x + 7 = 19` | `x + 7 = 19` |
| Hint | Add the notebook price x and the pen price. Their sum is the total paid. | 把筆記簿價錢 x 和筆價相加，便等於總價。 |
| Worked check | `x + 7 = 19`; if `x = 12`, then `12 + 7 = 19`. | `x + 7 = 19`；代入 `x = 12` 驗算，`12 + 7 = 19`。 |

Online scoring accepts `x+b=c`, its equality-reversed form, and `b+x=c` for addition, with optional spaces, full-width characters and the typographic minus sign. It rejects a value for `x` alone, a solved expression `x=c−b`, altered amounts, and equations that reverse subtraction. On paper, a teacher can assess an equivalent equation written in another valid layout using the same relationship; the online marker recognises the listed canonical arrangements. The answer key gives the contextual relationship and a substitution check. Practice starts with the addition story, then gives a subtraction story and a distinct addition follow-up; it records first tries separately and gives one targeted hint before a retry.
