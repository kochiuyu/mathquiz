## Actual pupil investigations

[Bilingual pupil notebook](../activities/p1-inquiry.html) · [Teacher facilitation notes](../activities/p1-inquiry.html?key=1). Default printing excludes notes; the toggle reveals them separately. No answer list or target count is supplied to pupils as a discovery shortcut. Hidden teacher notes include reference counts for checking records after exploration, not for announcing a target.

### A · Two trays, eight counters

Connection: 1N1 composition, optionally 1N2 addition discussion. Materials: nine large counters, two trays or drawn circles and recording paper; start with eight counters. Pupils predict the number of arrangements, choose whether swapped trays count as different, then physically split eight into two nonempty groups. They organise trials, record/check quantities, consider repeats or omissions, communicate how a partner could check their claim and revise it if needed. They then choose a total of six or nine, predict and test a transfer of their idea.

A way is defined by the two tray counts, not counter identity or arrangement within a tray. Equal-count swaps never produce a new way under either rule. Hidden teacher reference checks are: total 8 has 7 ordered or 4 unordered positive-part pairs; total 6 has 5 or 3; total 9 has 8 or 4. In particular, 4 + 4 and 3 + 3 must not be double-counted. These counts are not pupil targets.

The choice of swap rule is deliberately explicit. Teachers must not silently mark against a different definition of “different”. Positive one-digit parts keep the task within 1N1; there is no required algebraic formula, general proof or target speed. A complete enumeration can be discussed through concrete arrangements rather than asserted as a formal proof requirement.

### B · Same strip, different counts?

Connection: 1M1 improvised-unit measurement, with counting from 1N1. Teacher prepares strips of 12 cm and 18 cm and ample tiles of 2 cm and 3 cm, without revealing centimetre labels to pupils. These are preparation dimensions, not a conversion or multiplication task. Both unit choices exactly cover both lengths. Alternative preparations must preserve this whole-unit condition.

Pupils predict whether the two tile sizes give equal counts, decide what to keep unchanged, measure the same strip with each set, record counts and placement, repeat to check, and explain whether evidence supports their prediction. They test a second strip and offer evidence-based advice for reporting a length to another class. The teacher does not announce the unit-size relationship in advance.

## Objective-to-process evidence

These rows unpack one integrated aim; they are not invented additional EDB objectives. Each is authored evidence pending review/QA.

| Process aspect | Pupil action | Teacher observation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inquire and plan | State the question; choose a swap rule in A or fair-comparison plan in B; pose a next question | Record the child's rule/plan and any prompts, rather than crediting copied instructions. |
| Discover through evidence | Construct and check trials, organise records, search for omissions or repeat a measurement | Compare records against the actual counters or tile placements; note untested claims. |
| Communicate | Explain a method so a partner can reconstruct/check it; use speech, drawings or objects | Record a statement, demonstration or partner check, not writing volume. |
| Reason | Connect a claim to examples, respond to a possible repeat, missing case or conflicting result | Ask for another test without supplying the conclusion; observe whether evidence changes the claim. |
| Build/transfer concepts | Predict and test a different total or another strip; describe what stayed the same or changed | Record whether the new prediction uses an earlier observation and whether the pupil checks it. |

Teacher notes provide non-leading prompts and an independent/supported/not-yet/unobserved process rubric, with space for concrete evidence and next steps. Revision is recorded as part of reasoning, not punished as an initial error. No fixed pass score, required essay, multiplication/division, ratio, inequality notation or formal theorem is added. Safe large counters and simple table-top materials keep access age-appropriate; an adult may transcribe pupil speech without treating it as independent numeral writing.
