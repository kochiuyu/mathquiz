# P1 numbers to 100: live tasks and remaining objectives

**Source check:** 16 November 2026. The [EDB 2017 supplement, 1N3 objective 3](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/pmc2017_e.pdf#page=13) requires comparing number magnitudes and reserves `<` and `>` for 2N1. The [2018 Key Stage 1 notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=11) also describe comparing tens, then units, and ordering groups of numbers. The first release covered choosing the larger of **two** numerals only.

**Further source check:** The same [2017 supplement](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/pmc2017_e.pdf#page=13) and [2018 notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=11) specify counting, reading and writing numbers 21–100 (objective 1), understanding tens and units (objective 2), and counting objects in groups of 2, 5 and 10 without requiring calculation (objective 4). They also require odd/even recognition, comparing a group of numbers and estimating quantities below 100. The new tasks below cover only the stated slices.

## Bilingual item brief

English: “Which number is larger, 42 or 37? Choose the number.” Chinese: “哪個數較大：42還是37？選出該數。” Both values are between 21 and 100, distinct, and displayed in their spoken order. The pupil selects one numeral; no inequality symbol is requested. Twelve permanent `compare100a` cases include same-tens decisions, reversed digit pairs, tens-boundary pairs and 100 versus 90. One mark requires the larger displayed numeral. No partial credit.

The key compares tens first; when tens match, it compares units. The practice hint points to the relevant place without revealing the answer, then allows one retry and shows the worked step. Three distinct cases record correct first attempts separately. A wrong answer may mean a pupil compared only units; that is a hypothesis, not an observed misconception.

## Scope and next objectives

`tensones1a` (12 variants) shows bundles of ten counters and loose counters. The pupil writes the two-digit numeral, including zero-ones cases. It assesses reading a tens-and-units representation for objectives 1–2. It does not assess independently writing a spoken numeral or recognising 100.

`countgroups1a` (12 variants) shows separate boxes of 2, 5 or 10 counters, with fewer than 100 counters. The pupil counts the collection; the key lists the successive group totals. This is a pictorial group-counting task for objective 4 and does not require a multiplication expression.

`sequence100a` (12 variants) asks for the next number when counting forwards or backwards by one, including crossings of tens and the endpoint 100. It assesses part of objective 1, not counting in groups.

Each family uses one exact whole-number numeral for one mark, including full-width digits, with a three-question practice route, a targeted hint, one retry and a worked step. The picture descriptions name visible groups without giving their total. Misconceptions such as ignoring a zero in the units place or counting boxes rather than counters are hypotheses, not observed errors.

**Next objective slices:** the first release left odd/even recognition, ordering groups of numerals, estimation and number-word writing open. The release below addresses fixed written and pictured forms of those slices. A full-unit claim still requires objective-by-objective review and suitable tasks or a teacher-led rubric for each objective.

## Four further released task forms · 16 November 2026

The [2017 supplement](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/pmc2017_e.pdf#page=13) and [2018 explanatory notes](https://www.edb.gov.hk/attachment/en/curriculum-development/kla/ma/curr/EN_KS1_e.pdf#page=11) support these four additional twelve-variant families.

- `oddeven100a` asks pupils to classify a displayed two-digit numeral as odd or even. The worked step inspects the units digit; both endings 0 and 9 occur. The bilingual choices show words rather than English-only code values.
- `ordernums100a` asks pupils to **write** three distinct numerals in ascending or descending order, one per field. A complete correct order earns one mark. Same-tens cases and 100 are included; inequality symbols are not used.
- `numberwords100a` asks pupils to convert printed English or Chinese number words into numerals, including tens with zero units and 100. It assesses reading words and writing numerals. It does **not** test hearing spoken numbers, pronunciation or listening accuracy; those remain outside the automated task.
- `estimate100a` shows a labelled reference box of ten counters beside an unlabelled collection of 18–84 counters. Pupils choose the nearest ten among three estimates; answer position varies. This provides a finite, reproducible estimation task. As with any multiple-choice estimate, it does not prove that a child used the reference rather than counted the dots. The picture's accessibility description describes the reference and target without giving the target count.

All four use permanent IDs, bilingual builder, pupil, paper and key views, and three-question practice with a hint, retry and first-try count. The unit remains **partially covered** pending objective-by-objective review, especially oral counting, spoken-number listening and performance beyond these fixed representations. Manual classroom, translation, physical printer and screen-reader checking remains deferred at the user's request.
