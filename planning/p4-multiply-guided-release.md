# P4 multiplication by two digits

25 November 2026. [Learner entry](../p4-learn.html?path=multiply). Six bilingual lessons, twelve guided examples, 54 practice tasks (three at each difficulty per lesson), and twelve separate check tasks. Guided coverage: 4N1.1–3; 4F1 inquiry is integrated, not an extra numbered objective. All later P4 guided topics remain planned. Next: division by two digits.

## Teaching and evidence

| Objective | Lessons and responses | Practical evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4N1.1 | Tens, splitting, carrying, three-digit factors; numeric totals and separately labelled ones/tens/total fields | Value cards, a split array, original columns and carry explanations |
| 4N1.2 | All three factors retained; any of the three first pairs accepted with its correct final total | Original prediction before grouping trials; compare two methods and explain a choice |
| 4N1.3 | Equal-group stories with units; calculation and a separate estimate-first activity | Make/draw a story, write its calculation and unit; compare estimate with exact product |

Models progress one step at a time. Tens products retain their zero place; carry messages name the units. Both two-digit × three-digit orders occur. The upper example is 999 × 99 = 98901. Screen models are not physical measurement or observed column writing.

Question bounds are explicit: two-factor numeric fields allow 0–99999; three-factor grouping fields allow 0–1000000 independently. Estimates allow 0–999999, including estimates greater than the exact two-factor maximum. Full-width digits and leading zeros normalize; signs, decimals, scientific notation, commas, blanks and out-of-range entries are rejected. Field order matters. First attempts and one supported retry are separate; any help left blank remains unknown. These tasks do not require property terminology.

The twelve check number situations were compared in normalized factor order against the lesson examples, guided tasks, practice tasks, model examples and exposed activity calculations. None overlaps; the check itself has twelve distinct situations. It includes free numeric answers, partial rows, a misplaced tens-row correction and unlabelled-operation stories. Revisiting repeats the sample; the bank's wider generated inventory is not claimed to be entirely novel.

398 × 27 has no exact-result element until an estimate and reasoning have been saved, followed by confirmation of paper calculation. The original estimate survives reveal and later comparison. This is a teaching sequence, not an anti-cheating barrier: answers are in downloadable client code. The grouping model opens trials only after saving the original question, prediction and plan. Written, oral or adult-transcribed practical evidence is labelled separately from automatic answer matches.

## Learner experience and records

Shared year/language/pupil controls now include P4. Its single implemented topic is searchable and has Learn → Try together → Practise, with three separate difficulty choices. A nonexistent P4 application review is not advertised. P3 multiplication/review remain optional revisits. Short pupil instructions come first, hints and writing are optional, and required inputs remain visible.

P4 has its own versioned grade/topic/pupil namespace, `mathquiz-p4-guided-v1-multiply-0` through `-2`, and separate `test` keys. It does not reuse or migrate P1–P3 or bank marks. The inherited conflict/draft contract blocks stale writes, protects malformed/future/oversized records, labels volatile storage and limits records to 524288 characters. Reset explicitly confirms the current topic/pupil only. Saved first answers, retries, estimates, practical notes, inquiry and cursors round-trip through reload. No mastery completion is inferred from a correct total or a self-recorded lesson visit.
