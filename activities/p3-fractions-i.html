P3 · Parts of one whole

Name ______ Date ______ · Use equal-sized paper strips, 12 large counters and paper/pencil. Compare equal-sized wholes; counters are not food.

1 · Equal parts, reading and notation

Fold a strip into four equal parts and mark one. Read and write its fraction, identify numerator, denominator and fraction line, and explain them. Split another strip into two unequal parts. Someone calls the smaller piece “one half”: explain the problem using equal parts, not merely a count of two pieces.

2 · A set can be one whole

Circle a set of 12 identical counters as one whole and select three. Say what fraction of the set is selected, draw and write it, explaining what the denominator counts. Select none, then all twelve; read/write each fraction and its value.

Equivalent fractions and comparison

3 · Find equal amounts physically

Fold three equal-sized strips into 2, 4 and 6 equal parts, marking 1, 2 and 3 parts. Align/overlay them, read/write the fractions with equality signs and explain equal value despite different part counts. Do not substitute a multiply-top-and-bottom rule. Mark an entire strip and compare with one whole; write a fraction equal to 1.

4 · Same denominator or numerator

Using equal-sized strip diagrams, explain aloud then record <, > or =: 2/5 versus 4/5; 1/3 versus 1/6; 2/3 versus 2/5; 3/4 versus 3/4. Fold more strips to check. Explain why more equal parts mean smaller parts when the numerator is the same. Do not compare fractions using different-sized wholes.

From diagrams to addition

5 · Explain orally before an equation

Each diagram is one whole strip. A, B and C mark non-overlapping parts used by different people. First say each person’s fraction and the total used, then write a same-denominator addition equation. You may keep the original denominator; simplest form is not required.

6 · Make another example

Design another equal-part strip shared by two or three people with no overlap and no more than one whole used. Ask a partner to explain orally, write the equation and check.

From diagrams to subtraction

7 · How much remains?

In III, X together with A and B makes the original 7/8 of a strip. Remove the two A parts, then the three B parts; X marks what remains. Explain the remainder orally, then write a subtraction equation with three fractions. In IV, start with the four X parts and remove all of them. What remains? Explain a fraction representing zero.

8 · Check the whole and equal parts

Show one equivalence, comparison and operation example, identifying its whole and equal parts. Why do denominators not get added/subtracted? Explain with the diagram. An answer such as 2/8 need not be simplified; if you know an equivalent form, explain it with a picture.

Teacher · Key and feedback

Provide at least six equal-sized strips, reusable/redrawable diagrams, 12 identical large counters and pencils. Six equal parts may be made by thirds then halving; provide checked pre-partitioned strips if needed and record assistance. Do not expect accurate thirds by eye alone.

1: 1/4; numerator counts selected equal parts, denominator counts all equal parts of the whole. Two unequal pieces do not justify “half”. 2: 3/12; 0/12=0; 12/12=1, with the full set as whole. 3: 1/2=2/4=3/6 by overlay of equal-sized wholes; full strips give 2/2,4/4,6/6=1. No expansion/reduction procedure.

4: <, >, >, =. Same denominator: compare selected part counts. Same numerator with equal wholes: larger denominator means smaller parts. 5: 2/8+3/8=5/8; 1/6+2/6+2/6=5/6. 7: 7/8−2/8−3/8=2/8; 4/5−4/5=0/5=0. All operands, intermediate values and answers are at most one. Accept justified equivalent answers; simplest form is not required. Do not teach common-denominator algorithms or a combined-numerator fraction procedure here.

Teacher · Observation and scope

Record independent / prompted / not yet / not observed with objects, diagrams, words and equations; record assistance separately.

4

5

Explains diagrams orally before writing pure addition/subtraction of up to three like-denominator fractions.

6

Checks through diagrams; correct numbers alone are insufficient.

Fractions stay from 0 to 1. No improper/mixed fractions, expansion/reduction algorithms, unlike-denominator operations or mixed addition/subtraction in one expression. Later-grade local draft pending independent, mobile/print and translation checks.

小三 · 一個整體的部分

姓名 ______ 日期 ______ · 同樣大小的紙條、12 粒大圓片、紙筆。用同一大小整體作比較；圓片不是食物。

1 · 等分、讀寫及術語

摺紙條成四等份，標記其中一份。讀出並寫成分數，指出分子、分母及分數線，說明各表示甚麼。另把紙條分成一大一小兩份；有人把小份叫「一半」，用等分要求解釋問題。不要只數有兩份。

2 · 一組物件也是整體

把 12 粒同樣圓片圈成一組，這一組是整體，選其中 3 粒。說所選佔整組幾分之幾，畫圖並寫分數，說清分母數甚麼。再選 0 粒及全部 12 粒，分別寫及讀出分數和值。

等值分數及比較

3 · 以實物找相同份量

取三條同樣大小的紙條，分別摺成 2、4、6 等份，標記 1、2、3 份。對齊疊合比較，讀寫三個分數及等號，解釋為何份數不同仍同值。不要只用分子分母乘同一數的規則。再把其中一條全部標記，與完整紙條比較，寫一個值是 1 的分數。

4 · 同分母或同分子

用同樣大小的紙條圖先口述比較，再寫＜、＞或＝：2/5 與 4/5；1/3 與 1/6；2/3 與 2/5；3/4 與 3/4。可另摺紙驗證。解釋同分子時為何分得越多，每份反而越小。不可用大小不同的完整紙條比較。

8 · 檢查整體及等分

展示一個等值、比較及運算例子，指出每題的完整整體和等分。為何加減時分母不用相加或相減？用圖解釋。答案如 2/8 不需化簡；如你已知同值寫法，也須能用圖解釋。

教師 · 解答與回饋

提供至少六條同尺寸紙條，可輪流重畫／使用，12 粒同款大圓片，紙筆；摺六等份可先把紙條分三等份再對摺，教師可協助但記錄援助。不要求學生純靠眼力準確三等分；可提供已核對的等分紙條。

1：1/4；分子是所選等份數，分母是完整整體的等份總數。兩份不相等不能只因有兩份而稱一半。2：3/12；0/12＝0；12/12＝1，整組是整體。3：1/2＝2/4＝3/6，以同大小紙條對齊作證；全條可寫 2/2、4/4 或 6/6＝1。不教擴分／約分程序。

4：＜、＞、＞、＝。同分母比所取份數；同分子在相同整體下，分母越大每等份越小。5：2/8＋3/8＝5/8；1/6＋2/6＋2/6＝5/6。7：7/8－2/8－3/8＝2/8；4/5－4/5＝0/5＝0。所有過程及結果都不大於 1。接受有圖解的同值答案，不要求最簡形式，也不教通分或把分子合併成一個分式的運算程序。

教師 · 觀察及範圍

記獨立／提示後／尚未／未觀察，加實物、圖、語句及橫式，分開記錄協助。

1

指出整體和等分，分別以物件及一組物件表達分數。

2

讀寫分數並解釋分子、分母、分數線及 0／1。

3

以實物／圖說明等值，不只背擴分規則。

4

比較同分母或同分子分數並解釋。

5

先口述圖意，再寫最多三個同分母分數的純加法／純減法。

6

以圖核對，不把正確數字當作全部理解證據。

只用 0 至 1 的分數；不教假分數、帶分數、擴分約分程序、異分母運算或同一橫式加減混合。後續年級本地草稿，待獨立、手機／列印及翻譯檢查。
