B1 · Ask what rounding may hide

Choose a question: can different exact counts look tied after rounding? Can adding rounded categories differ from rounding their exact total? B3 · Challenge, qualify and transfer

Ask a partner to interpret your display and check whether its wording says “about”. Record their actual response, including no response. Revise your claim, explain what rounding hides, and transfer to new data or a nearest-hundred rule. A documented contradicted or unresolved conjecture is useful evidence. Under the same rounding rule, unequal nonnegative counts may become tied, but their order cannot reverse. Keep a bounded conclusion rather than claiming rounding always changes a story.

Teacher guidance and bounded reference checks

Teacher reference only, not pupil observations. Area 24 has four unordered positive-integer rectangle cases. Count cell area and exposed border edges separately. Among these four, perimeter 20 is smallest; 1 by 24 versus 4 by 6 is a counterexample to equal-area/equal-perimeter. This is not an optimisation claim for non-integer sides or all polygons. With perimeter 24, widths 1–6 and complementary heights 11–6 give six unordered cases; greatest area 36 is within this finite set only. Accept a partial search as partial, not exhaustive.
