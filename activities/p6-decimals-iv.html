1 · Place-value investigation

Use counters or a place-value chart to model 4.8 divided by 10,100,1000. Predict before moving or renaming units, then check by multiplication. Create a new whole-number example.

2 · Small divisors

Cut a paper strip representing 1.2 m into pieces representing 0.1 m. Predict the number, make the model, then explain division by 0.01 and 0.001 using a place-value chart. Record what you predict and what you observe when cutting the strip.

3 · Four division combinations

Solve and explain: 1.5÷2,3÷4,3÷0.5,1.2÷0.3. Draw a sharing or measurement model and check every quotient by multiplication. Explain why multiplying both dividend and divisor by the same power of ten preserves the quotient.

4 · Order and approximation

Compare (1.2+0.8)÷2×3 and 1.2+0.8÷2×3. Show the first operation in each. Then calculate 5÷3 to the nearest tenth and hundredth, using ≈ rather than = for rounded results. Estimate before calculating.

5 · Whole purchases

Each pen costs $14.8. With $60, predict the greatest whole number of pens and money remaining. Use play money, show why one more pen is unaffordable, then choose your own safe classroom supply example.
