Use place-value materials, drawings or written work to explain. Complete exact-calculation practice before the estimation section. All written calculations stay within three-digit whole numbers; money is expressed in dollars and cents, not decimal operations.

A · Three-digit subtraction

Work out 432 − 187 and 500 − 268. Show your working on paper. Use a model or drawing to explain a regrouping you used. Which quantities changed their grouping, and which total stayed the same?

C · Choose operations for a story

A library has 365 books on a shelf. Pupils borrow 128, then return 47 to that shelf. How many are on the shelf now? Write the steps and explain what each step finds.

A class has 215 stickers, receives 136 more, then uses 84. How many remain? Write the steps and explain them.

D · Give change with dollars and cents

A ruler costs 3 dollars 80 cents. A pupil pays 10 dollars. Find the change. Use coins, value cards or a drawing, and record the answer in dollars and cents:

A pupil has 20 dollars, spends 6 dollars 70 cents on a notebook, then 4 dollars 50 cents on a pen. How much remains? Show the amount after each purchase and explain your method.

If you regroup, explain why 1 dollar can be represented by 100 cents. Do not write a decimal calculation. These are pretend purchases; no real payment is made.

E · Estimate, then check

Use this after you have practised exact addition and subtraction. Before calculating exactly, choose nearby easy numbers and explain your approximate result. Then calculate and compare.

Teacher setup, answers and observable rubric

Prerequisites: 2N4 → 2M3 → this unit. Supply place-value mats, ten hundred blocks, twenty tens rods and twenty ones, or equivalent drawings; paper; and teacher-made money value cards including dollar and cent amounts. For D, coins may supplement the cards but authentic currency recognition is not assessed here. Value cards must clearly show units. Teacher may model the value-preserving 1 dollar/100 cents regrouping. Do not require real purchases, a particular written borrowing mark or a formal property name.

TaskEvidence and expected results
A432 − 187 = 245; 500 − 268 = 232. A possible regrouping of 432 is 3 hundreds, 12 tens, 12 ones; of 500 is 4 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones. Both conserve the starting value. Accept other correct methods, including partitioning or counting on, if explained. Addition checks: 245 + 187 = 432; 232 + 268 = 500. Observe the explanation rather than infer it from a correct numeral.
BFirst/final results: 423/227; 335/482; 363/678; 574/389. Addition-only and subtraction-only three-number expressions are included in this unit scope. The mistaken first step in the mixed example does not follow left-to-right; start 620 − 285 = 335, then add 147. Do not introduce multiplication/division precedence or ask pupils to handle negative results.
CLibrary: 365 − 128 = 237 after borrowing; 237 + 47 = 284 after returns. Stickers: 215 + 136 = 351 after receiving; 351 − 84 = 267 left. Ask pupils to connect each operation to its event and include the unit. These are sequential change stories, not a comparison (“more/fewer than”) followed by an altogether question.
DChange: 6 dollars 20 cents. One method represents 10 dollars as 9 dollars 100 cents, then removes 3 dollars 80 cents. For the second task, after notebook: 13 dollars 30 cents; after pen: 8 dollars 80 cents. Regroup 13 dollars 30 cents as 12 dollars 130 cents when needed. Counting up is also valid. Written work may use dollars/cents words or two labelled columns; do not require or teach decimal subtraction. Check value conservation and units, not just final numbers.
EIllustrative estimates: 400 + 200 − 100 = about 500; 600 − 300 = about 300. Exact answers 502 and 305. Other sensible nearby choices accepted with explanation. Record estimate before exact work; an exact answer copied into the estimate box is not estimation evidence. No fixed error percentage or compulsory rounding convention.

Source limit: no operand or intermediate result in the whole-number tasks exceeds 999. In particular, a sum reaching four digits before a later subtraction is excluded even if the final answer is three-digit. Monetary amounts use separate units; internal digital cent storage is not a pupil decimal-operation task.

C · 為情境選運算

書架原有365本書，學生借走128本，然後把47本還到同一書架。現在多少本？寫步驟，解釋每步求甚麼。

班上原有215張貼紙，收到136張，再用了84張。還剩多少？寫步驟並解釋。

D · 用元和仙計找贖

一把尺售3元80仙，付10元，應找多少？用硬幣、金額卡或圖畫表示，答案寫元和仙：

原有20元，先用6元70仙買筆記簿，再用4元50仙買筆，還剩多少？展示每次購買後的餘額及解釋方法。

如需退位，說明1元為何可表示成100仙。不要寫小數算式。這是假設購物，不作真實付款。

教師準備、解答及觀察準則

先修次序：2N4 → 2M3 → 本單元。提供位值板、十塊百、二十條十、二十個一，或相應圖畫；紙及教師製作、清楚註明元／仙的金額卡。D可輔以硬幣，但此處不評估真幣辨認。教師可示範1元／100仙等值轉換。不要求真實購物、特定退位記號或正式性質名稱。

任務證據及預期結果
A432 − 187 = 245；500 − 268 = 232。432可轉成3個百、12個十、12個一；500可轉成4個百、9個十、10個一，均保持原值。接受其他正確且能解釋的方法，如拆數或往上數。加法核對：245 + 187 = 432；232 + 268 = 500。須觀察說明，不能只由正確答案推斷。
B第一步／最後：423／227；335／482；363／678；574／389。本範圍也包括三數全加或全減。錯誤混合題沒有從左至右，應先620 − 285 = 335，再加147。不加入乘除優先或負數。
C圖書：365 − 128 = 237為借出後，237 + 47 = 284為歸還後。貼紙：215 + 136 = 351為收到後，351 − 84 = 267為餘下。請把運算與事件連結，寫單位。這是連續變化，不是「比某人多／少」再求總和的後續題型。
D找6元20仙。例如把10元轉成9元100仙，再減3元80仙。第二題買簿後13元30仙，買筆後8元80仙；需要時把13元30仙轉成12元130仙。往上數也可。可寫元／仙文字或兩個有單位的直欄，不要求或教小數減法。核對等值及單位，不只看最後數字。
E估計例子：400 + 200 − 100約500；600 − 300約300。準確答案502及305。接受其他合理附近數及解釋。先記估計再精算，把精算答案抄作估計不顯示估計過程。不設固定誤差百分比或指定進捨法。

來源限制：整數題各數及中間結果都不超過999。先加得四位數再減回三位數也不在範圍內。金額分單位處理；程式內用整數仙儲存不是學生的小數運算。
