P6 · Inquiry and investigation

1 · Frame a real decision

Plan how to allocate a school display area or time at an event. Choose the decision, users and constraints; do not simply copy a fixed numerical problem.

P6 · Inquiry and investigation

2 · Select evidence and assumptions

A teacher may supply a safe site sketch, time budget or aggregate counts. Record which information you need, its source and assumptions you choose. Leave unknowns visible.

P6 · Inquiry and investigation

3 · Build a simple representation

Choose a table, scale drawing, chart or number sentence showing the relevant quantities. Explain how your assumptions simplify the situation.

P6 · Inquiry and investigation

4 · Solve and interpret

Use known mathematics to compare possible arrangements. Interpret the answer in context, including whole items or available time. Keep calculations and units.

P6 · Inquiry and investigation

5 · Check against reality

With teacher guidance compare your proposal with a safe observation or supplied constraint. State a strength and a limitation; a second complete modelling cycle is optional.

P6 · Inquiry and investigation

6 · Communicate and reflect

Explain your choice to a partner and record their question, your response and any revision. Use a process rubric, not a prefilled mastery or hours-delivered claim.

Teacher models and observation record

No single numerical answer is prescribed. Assess the stated question, appropriate evidence, reasonable assumptions, mathematics, interpretation and communication. The 2026 provisional modelling guidance is forward-looking: planned KS2 implementation 2028/29, with earlier pilots possible; it does not replace current core topic scope. Guided first-cycle modelling may focus on the first four stages with a light check. AI, if used, follows independent pupil thinking with teacher supervision and output checking.
