P2 · Organise and represent data

Name ______ Date ______ · Paper, pencil, scissors used by an adult, and these twelve object cards. The cards describe a small model collection, not a survey of the class.

1 · Before and after sorting

Before cutting or marking, try to find which item has most cards. What makes this hard to check? Now ask an adult to cut the cards, mix them on the table, then sort into the three labelled groups. Count each card once. Record each total and explain how grouping helps you check.

2 · One symbol for one card

Use the sorted cards. Write a suitable title. In each row draw one complete ○ for every card in that category, starting at the left, with equal-sized symbols in successive cells. Keep category labels and the key. Leave unused cells empty; the cells themselves are not symbols. Point from each card to its matching symbol and check nothing was omitted or counted twice.

3 · Read your representation

What do the title, category labels and key tell you? How many book cards? Which category has most, and which fewest? How many more pencil cards than book cards? Explain by matching symbols, not merely by looking at the occupied space.

Construct and read a vertical pictogram

4 · Same data, different form

Use the SAME cards, not new observations. Write a title and draw a vertical pictogram: start at the bottom of each labelled column and build upward, one complete ○ per card, with equal-sized symbols. Keep the one-to-one key. Verify each column against the sorted cards.

5 · Compare, check and change

Read the ball count from BOTH charts. Have the quantities changed just because the chart turned? Explain why a title, categories and key matter. On a separate copy, remove every book card and redraw that category: how do you show zero without deleting its label? Do not change the original charts. Explain why drawing one ○ for two cards, or stretching symbols to make a row longer, would not follow these charts’ rules.

Teacher · Setup and key

Use the supplied 12 word cards or replace them with corresponding safe objects: 5 pencils, 3 books and 4 balls. Cards avoid needing large classroom inventories. Supply spare paper for zero-category transfer. Adult cuts; no personal information collection is needed. If using objects, change titles/key consistently to one object rather than one card. Read word labels aloud if needed and record assistance.

Task 1: elicit difficulty in unorganised comparison before sorting. Sorted counts are pencil 5, book 3, ball 4. Do not announce totals beforehand. Tasks 2 and 4: require the pupil to construct both orientations, not copy a ready chart. Accept any clear title such as “Our object cards”; three labelled categories; key one circle per card; totals 5/3/4. Equal-size aligned symbols allow comparison, but counting and correspondence determine correctness.

Task 3: books 3, pencils most, books fewest; pencils exceed books by 2. Task 5: balls 4 in both; orientation does not change data. A zero book category remains labelled with no symbols. One symbol for two cards violates the stated one-to-one key; decorative or stretched marks must not imply extra objects. No scaled keys, half-symbols or fractional data required.

Teacher · Observable rubric

For each row record independent / prompted / not yet / not observed, with date and actual pupil actions or words. Ask “Show where that card appears” and “How could someone check?” before correcting.

  1. Explains an organisational difficulty and sorts/counts without omissions or duplicates.
  2. Identifies title, category and one-to-one key.
  3. Reads and compares counts in both orientations, including a retained empty category.
  4. Personally constructs horizontal AND vertical pictograms with matching data, equal-sized symbols, labels and a key.
  5. Checks representation against source cards and explains why orientation changes no totals.

Construction, sorting and explanation are not proved by a correct digital count. These are resource tasks, not claims of pupil mastery. Native translation, browser/mobile/print and classroom verification remain pending. Sources: EDB supplement PDF 40–41; KS1 notes PDF 47.

3 · 解讀你的圖

5 · 比較、覆核及改變

從兩幅圖讀出球卡的數目。轉了方向，數量會改變嗎？解釋標題、類別及圖例為何重要。在另紙移去所有書本卡後重畫該類：如何保留類別名稱而表示零？不要改原圖。說明為何一個 ○ 代表兩張卡，或把圖案拉長使一行較長，都不符合本圖規則。

教師 · 準備及解答

用所附十二張文字卡，或相應安全實物：五枝鉛筆、三本書、四個球。用卡可免準備大量實物。另備紙作零類別延伸，成人剪卡；不收集個人資料。如用實物，標題及圖例須改為每個圖案代表一件實物。可協助讀卡上文字，但須記錄。

活動 1 先讓學生說未整理時的困難，再分類；鉛筆 5、書本 3、球 4，不預先告知總數。活動 2、4 須學生親自製作兩種方向，不是抄現成圖。標題清楚即可，例如「我們的物件卡」；保留三類名稱、一圓代表一卡的圖例及 5／3／4 數量。相同大小、排列整齊有助比較，但正確性須用數數及一一配對驗證。

活動 3：書本 3，鉛筆最多，書本最少；鉛筆比書本多 2。活動 5：兩圖球卡均 4；方向不改數據。零書本類仍有名稱，但沒有圖案。一個圖案代表兩張卡違反本圖一對一圖例；不可用裝飾或拉長符號暗示更多物件。不要求倍數圖例、半個圖案或分數數據。

教師 · 可觀察準則

每項記錄獨立／提示後／尚未／未觀察，附日期及實際動作或說話。回饋前問「這張卡在圖中哪裏？」和「別人怎樣覆核？」

  1. 說明未整理時的困難，分類及數數不漏不重。
  2. 指出標題、類別及一對一圖例。
  3. 解讀及比較兩種方向，包括保留名稱的零類別。
  4. 親自製作橫向和直向象形圖，數據一致、圖案大小相同，附名稱及圖例。
  5. 用原卡覆核圖，解釋轉向不改總數。
