P3 · Heavy and light

Name ______ Date ______ · Use safe light objects, an equal-arm classroom beam balance, identical counters/blocks and suitable weighing scales. Adult checks equipment and load limits. No people, pets, sharp objects, food tasting or lifting heavy loads.

1 · What does weight mean?

Lift a light empty pencil case, then the same case with a few pencils. Describe the change using heavier/lighter. Explain why a larger-looking object is not always heavier: compare a large sponge with a small safe solid object selected by the teacher.

2 · Predict by feeling

The teacher provides two safe objects with clearly different weights. Hold one in each hand, low over the table; predict which is heavier. Swap hands and check your feeling. Record the prediction and reason before using a tool.

3 · Direct comparison

Check that the empty equal-arm balance is level and free to move. Put one object in the centre of each pan, wait until still, and record which pan is lower or whether level. Explain the comparison. Swap the objects between pans and repeat to check. Each pupil handles the objects and explains the result.

5 · Why standard units?

Two groups balance the SAME object: one reports 4 large blocks, the other 8 small blocks. Explain why these counts do not show different weights of the object and why a shared standard helps. Handle safe labelled 1 g and 1 kg examples supplied by the teacher, on the table if needed. Match gram to g and kilogram to kg. A set of ten 100 g weights balances a 1 kg standard. Count the grams and explain the relationship. Do not infer weight from size alone.

Measure, estimate and record

6 · Choose and read a scale

Choose a suitable scale for a pencil case and another for a manageable bag of books. Explain your choice using range, smallest marked division and unit. Check zero on a flat surface; place the object centrally and wait. Read with your eyes aligned to the scale, avoiding a slanted view. On a digital scale wait for a stable reading and check the unit. If a tray is required, zero with the empty tray first under adult guidance. Measure both objects and compare using the same unit.

7 · Estimate after weighing experience

Use a measured object as a reference. Estimate the weight of a new safe object C BEFORE weighing; record your estimate, then measure and compare. Explain any revision. Between marked divisions, give a justified range rather than false exactness. Use whole-gram or whole-kilogram examples; decimal recording is not required here.

8 · Choose units and record

Choose g or kg: an eraser labelled 20 ___; a bag of rice labelled 5 ___. Explain the choice without lifting the rice bag. Record these measured weights in grams: 1 kg 30 g; 2 kg 250 g; 3 kg 5 g. Show how the kilograms and remaining grams combine. Read a safe everyday product label and copy its number and unit. Do not convert grams back to mixed units or use decimals.

Try recording weights in grams. A correct calculation does not prove that you can use a balance.

Teacher key and practical setup

Use everyday language “weight”, not a formal mass/force distinction. Keep loads within manufacturer limits. Check an empty equal-arm balance; choose clearly different weights for the first feeling comparison. For improvised units use genuinely equal-weight units, not merely similarly sized objects. Initial tasks can use exact whole-unit balance; later bracketing is recorded honestly.

  1. Adding pencils makes the case heavier; a bulky sponge can be lighter than a small solid object. Observe language and justification, not size guessing.
  2. Record the actual prediction before tools. Swapping hands reduces reliance on a stronger hand; no prescribed answer without the real objects.
  3. For a functioning equal-arm balance, the lower pan holds the heavier object; level pans indicate equal weights within the tool's sensitivity. Swap sides; disagreement prompts checking zero, placement and free movement.
  4. Accept suitable equal units and an explanation. Count the same chosen units for both objects. If an object lies between5 and6 units, record that range. Mixed unequal blocks cannot be added as if equal.
  5. The object is unchanged; units differ. g means gram, kg means kilogram. Ten100 g weights total1000 g, matching1 kg. Actual handling and labels establish reference experience.
  6. Assess tool choice, zero, unit, placement, reading and comparison separately. A unit mismatch invalidates a raw-number comparison. Actual outcomes depend on equipment; do not supply fictitious readings.
  7. After reference experience, estimate first, measure second. Credit reasoned use of a reference and revision; no arbitrary percentage tolerance.
  8. 20 g;5 kg. 1000+30=1030 g;2000+250=2250 g;3000+5=3005 g. Do not require reverse conversion or decimals.

小三 · 輕和重

姓名 ______ 日期 ______ · 使用安全輕便的物件、課室等臂天平、相同籌碼／積木及合適的秤。成人檢查工具及負重上限。不量人或寵物，不用尖銳物件，不試食，不提重物。

1 · 重量是甚麼？

拿起輕的空筆袋，再拿起放入幾枝筆的同一筆袋，用較重／較輕描述改變。比較教師選定的大海綿與小而安全的實心物件，解釋為何外形較大的物件不一定較重。

2 · 憑感覺預測

教師提供重量明顯不同的兩件安全物件。左右手各拿一件，靠近桌面，預測哪件較重。交換左右手再感受，先記下預測及理由，然後才用工具。

3 · 直接比較

先檢查空的等臂天平平衡且可自由移動。把兩件物件各放在一個秤盤中央，待穩定後記錄哪邊較低或是否平衡，解釋比較結果。把物件左右交換再量，以作核對。每位同學都要操作及說明。

5 · 為何用標準單位？

兩組量同一件物件，一組是4大積木，另一組是8小積木。為何這不表示物件重量不同？共同標準有甚麼用？觀察教師提供安全且有標示的1 g和1 kg例子，如有需要留在桌面操作。把克配對g，千克配對kg。十個100 g砝碼與1 kg標準砝碼平衡，點算克數並解釋關係。不可只憑大小判斷重量。

6 · 選秤及讀數

為筆袋選一個合適的秤，再為容易搬動的一袋書選另一個。用量程、最小分格及單位說明選擇。在平坦表面檢查零位，物件放中央，待穩定才讀。眼睛對準刻度，避免斜看。數碼秤須等讀數穩定，並查看單位。如需托盤，先在成人指導下連空托盤歸零。量兩件物件，用同一單位比較。

8 · 選單位及記錄

選g或kg：橡皮標示20 ___；一袋米標示5 ___。不提起米袋，解釋選擇。把1 kg 30 g、2 kg 250 g及3 kg 5 g的實測記錄改寫成克，說明千克和餘下克數如何合併。讀安全日常用品的標籤，抄下數字及單位。不要求把克反轉為複名數或用小數。

試做以克記錄重量練習。計算答對並不證明會用天平。

教師解答及實作準備

使用日常「重量」用語，不正式區分質量及力。遵守工具負重上限，檢查空的等臂天平。初次憑感覺比較選重量差別明顯的物件。自訂單位須真正等重，不只外形相似。初次可用整個單位剛好平衡的例子，其後介乎兩數之間須如實記錄。

  1. 加入鉛筆後筆袋較重，大海綿可以比小實心物件輕，觀察用語及解釋，不只猜大小。
  2. 先記預測，交換手減少慣用手影響；沒有實物不可預設答案。
  3. 正常等臂天平較低一邊較重；平衡表示在工具靈敏度內重量相等。換邊結果不一致，須檢查零位、擺放及自由移動。
  4. 接受合適的相等單位及理由，兩件物件用同一種單位。介乎5至6個單位就記範圍，不可把不等重積木當相等單位。
  5. 物件沒變，是單位不同。g是克，kg是千克。十個100 g合共1000 g，與1 kg相等。實際觀察及標示建立參考經驗。
  6. 分別評估選工具、零位、單位、擺放、讀數及比較。單位不同不能只比較數字。結果依實物，不提供虛構讀數。
  7. 有參考經驗後先估後量，評估理由及修正，不設武斷的百分比準確要求。
  8. 20 g及5 kg。1000＋30＝1030 g；2000＋250＝2250 g；3000＋5＝3005 g。不要求反向轉換或小數。
