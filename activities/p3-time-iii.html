P3 · Seconds in real activities

Name ______ Date ______ · Use a real clock with seconds, stopwatch or adult-set metronome, paper, six cards and pencil. Stay seated or at a clear table; no running or breath-holding.

1 · Experience a second

Watch the second hand or listen to evenly timed signals. Point to two successive signals to show one second. Find the symbol s on a timing tool. Experience a full minute and explain how many seconds it contains. Compare the feeling of one second and one minute; a second is not a minute.

2 · Estimate, measure and compare

Choose two safe short activities, such as making a simple paper fold and sorting six cards. Before timing, write an estimate in seconds for each. Agree exactly when each activity starts and finishes. You operate a real clock/stopwatch to measure while a partner performs, then swap roles. Record the displayed seconds, tool and any uncertainty. Compare durations; repeat a measurement if needed and explain differences. Actual measurements need not stay below 60 seconds. Keep original estimates.

Minutes: any missing quantity

3 · Find start, finish or duration

All these events occur on the same morning. (a) Reading ends at 10:15 and lasts 35 minutes: when did it start? (b) A game starts at 9:45 and lasts 30 minutes: when does it finish? (c) Music starts at 10:20 and ends at 11:00: how many minutes? Show a clock or time line and explain the direction of counting.

4 · Check a starting time

A lesson ends at 11:05 after 60 minutes. A pupil gives 11:65 as its start. Explain what is wrong, find the correct starting time and check by counting forward from your answer. No calculation with seconds is needed.

Hours and explanations

5 · Whole hours

All events occur on the same day. (a) A display closes at 5:00 p.m. after being open 8 hours: when did it open? (b) An event starts at 8:00 a.m. and lasts 12 hours: when does it end? (c) A class project starts at 9:00 a.m. and ends at 2:00 p.m.: how many hours? Label a.m./p.m. or noon as needed; do not assume every time is in the same half-day.

6 · Show and check

For one start-time answer, count forward by its given duration to recover the finish. For an elapsed-time answer, explain what the counted intervals represent. Explain why timing actual actions in seconds is different from these word problems using whole minutes or whole hours. Do not add decimal hours or second-based word problems.

Teacher · Setup, timing and answers

Use real timing tools. Adult may set a metronome to one beat per second (60 beats/minute), or use successive second-hand ticks. One interval between adjacent ticks is one second: counting ten intervals takes eleven tick positions if the initial tick is labelled zero. Pupils need not learn beats-per-minute notation. Count60seconds to experience one minute. Encourage estimates; do not announce target durations for Task2. Choose manageable actions (often roughly20–90seconds), not a required measurement range. Source imposes no fixed limit on actual timed measurements, unlike word problems.

Agree observable starts/stops; record actual display/readings and tool resolution. Prefer whole-second-display tools at this stage. If a tool has fractions, retain the observed reading with teacher support or use a whole-second display; do not silently relabel a fractional observation as an exact integer. Reaction delays and repeat differences are legitimate uncertainty, not a reason to invent prescribed results. Require each pupil to handle/read the timing tool, separately recording their partner’s performance.

Task3: (a)9:40 a.m.; (b)10:15 a.m.; (c)40minutes. Task4:10:05 a.m.; minute positions run0–59, and10:05 plus60minutes gives11:05. There is no11:65 clock reading. Task5: (a)9:00 a.m.; (b)8:00 p.m.; (c)5hours. Use noon continuity when crossing halves of the day. No24-hour notation required here; that belongs to3M4.

Teacher · Observable evidence

Record independent / prompted / not yet / not observed with actual readings, written timelines and pupil explanations.

  1. Shows a one-second interval and explains60seconds=1minute.
  2. Estimates before measuring, operates a real tool, records and compares seconds, with repeat/uncertainty explained.
  3. Finds starting time, finishing time and elapsed time in whole-minute contexts no greater than60minutes.
  4. Finds all three unknowns in whole-hour contexts no greater than12hours, keeping a.m./p.m. correct.
  5. Checks inverse answers by recovering the stated endpoint and explains the interval.

Digital choices do not prove physical timing or estimation. Keep sensible actual measurements separate from the restricted word-problem range. 小三 · 真實活動中的秒

姓名 ______ 日期 ______ · 用有秒針的鐘、秒錶或成人設定的節拍器、紙、六張卡及筆。坐下或在暢通桌邊操作，不跑步、不閉氣。

1 · 體驗一秒

看秒針或聽均勻訊號，指出相連兩次訊號之間是一秒。在計時工具找秒的符號 s。體驗一整分鐘，解釋有多少秒。比較一秒和一分鐘的感覺，兩者不相同。

2 · 先估、實測及比較

選兩項安全短活動，例如摺簡單紙摺及分類六張卡。計時前各寫以秒估計的時長，商定明確起止動作。你操作鐘／秒錶量同伴做活動的時間，再交換角色。記錄實際秒讀數、工具及不確定之處。比較時長，必要時重測，解釋差別。真實量度不必限於六十秒以下。保留原估值。

分鐘：找任何未知量

3 · 起時、終時及時長

以下均在同一上午。(a)閱讀10:15結束，歷時35分鐘，何時開始？(b)遊戲9:45開始，歷時30分鐘，何時結束？(c)音樂10:20開始，11:00結束，共多少分鐘？用鐘或時間線說明順數／倒數。

4 · 覆核開始時刻

一課11:05結束，歷時60分鐘。有同學說11:65開始，錯在哪裏？求正確起時，再由答案向前數給定時長覆核終時。不用以秒計算。

小時及解釋

5 · 整數小時

以下活動均在同一天。(a)展覽下午5:00關閉，開放8小時，何時開？(b)活動上午8:00開始，歷時12小時，何時結束？(c)專題上午9:00開始，下午2:00結束，共多少小時？按需要標示上午／下午／正午，不假定所有時刻在同一半天。

6 · 展示及覆核

選一起時答案，由該時刻向前數給定時長，回到終時。選一時長答案，說所數的間隔代表甚麼。解釋用秒量真實活動，與這些以整分鐘或整小時作的應用題有何不同。不加入小數小時或秒的應用題。

教師 · 準備、計時及答案

用真實工具。成人可把節拍器設每秒一拍（每分鐘60拍），或用秒針相連刻度。相連兩拍之間是一秒；初拍記零，十個間隔會有十一個拍點，不要求學生學每分鐘拍數。數60秒體驗一分鐘。活動2鼓勵估測，不先告知預定時長；選可管理動作，例如大約20–90秒，這不是必需範圍。來源沒有對真實量度設固定上限，不同於應用題限制。

商定可觀察起止，記實際讀數和工具精細程度。此階段宜用整秒顯示工具；若顯示小數，可在教師支援下保留實際讀數或改用整秒顯示，不把非整秒偷偷當成準確整數。反應延遲及重測差別是合理不確定性，不應虛構指定結果。每生須操作／讀量具，同伴做動作另記。

活動3：(a)上午9:40；(b)上午10:15；(c)40分鐘。活動4：上午10:05；分欄0至59，10:05加60分鐘得11:05，沒有11:65。活動5：(a)上午9:00；(b)晚上8:00；(c)5小時。跨上午／下午時說明正午連續性，不要求24小時制，那屬3M4。

教師 · 可觀察證據

記獨立／提示後／尚未／未觀察，附實際讀數、時間線及說話。

  1. 展示一秒間隔及解釋60秒＝1分鐘。
  2. 先估後量，親用工具，記錄及比較秒數，說明重測／不確定性。
  3. 在不超過60整分鐘的情境分別求起時、終時、時長。
  4. 在不超過12整小時的情境分別求三種未知量，保留上午／下午。
  5. 由起時覆核終時並解釋間隔。

數碼選擇不能證明實測及估測。合理真實量度與有限制應用題分開。後續年級本地草稿，小二完成前不發布。待獨立來源／數學／翻譯及手機／列印／課堂驗證。來源：補充文件PDF51；說明PDF58。
