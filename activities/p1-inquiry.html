P1 · Try, notice, explain

Inquiry notebook · Name Date

You can move objects, draw or speak while an adult records your words. Try your own idea before hearing someone else’s. Keep early guesses, even when you change them.

Investigation A · Two trays, eight counters

Take 8 counters. Put them into two trays, with at least one in each. How many different ways can you find? Make a guess before trying:

Choose a rule with your partner: does swapping the two trays count as a new way, or the same way? Circle: new / same. Keep that rule for your investigation. A way means the number in each tray, not which counters you used or how you arranged them. Swapping trays with equal counts does not make a new way under either rule.

Build one way. Record each part and check that all 8 counters are still there. Try another way. Decide how to organise your trials so you do not miss one or repeat one.

Investigation B · Same strip, different counts?

Your teacher gives you one strip and two sets of equal-sized tiles. Tiles in one set are smaller than those in the other. Predict: will the two sets give the same count when you measure the same strip? Why?

Decide how to make a fair comparison. Keep the strip the same. Use one tile set at a time, from the same starting end, touching end to end. Record what you actually find.

Teacher facilitation, not an answer sheet

Use A during or after 1N1 composition work, with optional 1N2 discussion. Use B with 1M1 improvised-unit measurement. These are two opportunities within mathematics teaching, not a separate course or the entire 10-hour allocation. Split into short sessions as needed. Give time to explore, compare ideas and revisit a claim.

Materials and setup

A: 9 large counters (start with 8), two trays or drawn circles, paper and pencils. For the initial question keep both parts positive and one-digit. Let the pair choose and state its swapping rule; do not silently change the meaning of “different”. B: two non-stretch strips, e.g. 12 cm and 18 cm long, plus ample equal tiles of two different lengths, e.g. 2 cm and 3 cm. Teacher prepares these sizes with a ruler; pupils need no centimetre labels here. Make both strips whole multiples of each chosen unit to avoid fractions. Provide enough tiles to measure end to end. Keep counters away from mouths.

Prompts that leave thinking to pupils

A: “What are you trying to find?” “Can you show one example?” “How will you remember what you tried?” “Does that fit your rule?” “What changes when you move one counter?” “Could there be one you missed?” “How could your partner check?” “Would you keep or change your claim?”

B: “What will stay the same?” “Which part will you change?” “Where will you start?” “How could you check that trial?” “What do you notice about the unit and the count?” “Does your explanation fit the other strip?” “What information would another class need?”

Do not provide a completed list of pairs, a target number of solutions or a rule about unit size before children investigate. Teacher-only check: with two nonempty trays, total 8 has 7 ordered count pairs or 4 pairs when swaps are treated as the same; total 6 has 5 or 3; total 9 has 8 or 4, respectively. Equal-count pairs such as 4 and 4 (or 3 and 3 for total 6) are never counted twice merely by swapping. Counter identities and arrangements within a tray do not create new ways. Keep these reference counts hidden from pupils; use them to check records, not as an upfront target. If stuck, offer an empty recording grid or ask them to show just one trial; note that support. Challenge a claim by inviting another test, not by supplying the conclusion.

Use I = independent, S = supported, N = not yet shown, U = not observed. Record a specific action or quotation, not just a tick.

Question/plan: states what to find and chooses a workable rule or test. Evidence: carries out and records actual trials; checks total or unit placement. Communication: explains a trial so a partner can reconstruct it. Reasoning: connects a claim to examples and a check; considers a missed case or conflicting result. Concept building: uses what was noticed to predict/test a new total or strip. Revision: keeps or changes a claim for a stated reason.

Question/plan __ Evidence __ Communication __ Reasoning __ Concept building __ Revision __. Describe any untested claim separately. Correct arithmetic alone does not demonstrate inquiry; an incorrect first prediction followed by a careful revision can provide strong process evidence. No fixed pass score, required written essay or speed target.

Check mathematical records against the child’s physical trials. A: both parts must match the trays and total the chosen collection; honour the stated swapping rule. B: verify consistent unit, aligned start, no gaps/overlaps and agreement between counted tiles and record. A pupil need not prove a general theorem or use algebra, division, multiplication, ratios or inequality signs.

探究B · 同一紙帶，數目不同？

老師給你一條紙帶和兩套方片，每套的方片大小相同，但一套比另一套小。先猜：量同一紙帶，兩套的數目會相同嗎？為甚麼？

決定怎樣公平比較，保持紙帶不變。每次用一套方片，從同一起點開始，首尾相接。記錄實際發現。

教師促進探究指引，不是答案表

A可配合1N1數的組合活動，可延伸討論1N2；B配合1M1自訂單位量度。這是數學課內的兩個探究機會，不是獨立課程，也不代表已完成全部10小時。可分短節進行，留時間探索、交流及重新檢視想法。

材料及準備

A：9個大圓片（先用8個）、兩盤或兩個畫出的圈、紙筆。初題兩部分均為正的一位數。讓學生訂明交換兩盤的規則，不要暗中改變「不同」的意思。B：兩條不易伸長的紙帶，例如長12和18厘米，以及足夠的兩套相同方片，例如一套長2厘米，另一套長3厘米。教師用尺準備，學生此題不需厘米標示。兩條紙帶均須可用任一套方片完整量度，以免引入分數。方片須足夠首尾相接排列。圓片不要放近口部。

讓學生自己想的提問

A：「你想找甚麼？」「可展示一個例子嗎？」「怎樣記住試過甚麼？」「這符合你們的規則嗎？」「移動一個圓片，甚麼會改變？」「會不會漏了一種？」「同伴怎樣核對？」「你會保留還是修改想法？」

B：「甚麼保持不變？」「你改變哪一項？」「從哪裏開始？」「怎樣核對？」「單位和數目有甚麼值得留意？」「另一紙帶也符合你的解釋嗎？」「另一班還需要甚麼資料？」

不要預先提供所有數對、答案總數或單位大小的規則。只供教師核對：兩盤均非空時，總數8按次序計有7種，交換視作相同則有4種；總數6分別有5種和3種；總數9分別有8種和4種。兩盤數量相同，例如4和4（總數6時的3和3），不能只因交換而重複計算。圓片的身份或盤內排列不構成新方法。不要向學生展示這些參考數目；用來核對記錄，而非預先指定目標。遇到困難，可提供空表或請學生先試一個例子，並記下支援。請學生再試來檢視想法，不直接告知結論。

提問／計劃：說明想找甚麼，選可行的規則或試驗。證據：實際試驗及記錄，核對總數或單位擺放。溝通：清楚說明，讓同伴能重現試驗。推理：用例子和核對支持想法，考慮遺漏或不符的結果。建立概念：把發現用於另一總數或紙帶，先猜再試。修訂：有理由地保留或修改想法。
