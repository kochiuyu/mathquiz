P3 · What can a container hold?

Name ______ Date ______ · Use clean unbreakable containers, a tray, water, towels and adult supervision. Do not drink the water. Do not use medicines, detergents, hot liquids or glass.

1 · Capacity and amount now

Point to a cup and a bucket. Explain what their capacity means. Put a little water in the cup, then add more without overflowing. Has its capacity changed, or just the amount of water in it? Draw or explain.

2 · Predict from a clear difference

Look at a small cup and a much larger jug. Without pouring yet, predict which can hold more when full. Explain what you noticed. Keep your prediction to compare with a test.

3 · A direct comparison

Choose two small containers A and B. Agree what full means (to the brim) and work over a tray. Fill A, then carefully pour all its water into empty B. Record whether B still has space, is exactly full, or overflows. What does this show about their capacities? If water spills outside B before it is full, repeat rather than count the spill as evidence. Swap roles so each pupil pours and explains.

4 · Choose an improvised unit

Choose either a small cup or a tiny spoon to measure a jug. Which is more suitable here? Explain your choice. Use the SAME chosen unit filled to the SAME level for both containers. Count full units needed to fill A and B; note any last partially filled unit rather than pretending it is full. Use only two containers at a time. Which holds more? 5 · Why standard units?

Two groups measure the same jug: one reports 4 large cups and the other 8 small cups. Has the jug changed capacity? Explain why the numbers alone cannot be compared. Suggest a common unit.

Handle a labelled 1 L vessel and a 100 mL measuring cup. With supervision, fill the 100 mL cup to its mark ten times and transfer each to the 1 L vessel. What total does this represent? Match litre with L or l, and millilitre with mL or ml. Explain why L is easier to distinguish from the numeral 1.

Measure, estimate and record

6 · Choose and use a measuring tool

Choose a measuring jug with a suitable range and readable small divisions for a small bottle, then for a larger jug. Explain why an unmarked cup or a tool too small for the full amount is less convenient. Fill each target container to the brim over a tray, then transfer its contents to the measuring tool without loss. Use repeated measured portions if needed; write every portion and add them.

Set the measuring tool level on a flat surface. Put your eyes near the water level; read the scale, not the top of the container. Record unit and smallest division. Measure two containers and compare. Distinguish a container's full capacity from the amount in it when partly filled.

8 · Record useful units

Choose L or mL and explain: a small drink carton labelled 250 ___; a large water container labelled 5 ___. A measured bottle holds 1 L 30 mL. Record its capacity in mL and explain the addition. Do the same for 2 L 250 mL and 3 L 5 mL. Do not use decimals. Read a safe clean drink-container label and copy the number and unit; distinguish labelled contents from maximum capacity.

Then try the existing measuring-cup quiz. Explain why a correct screen reading cannot prove that you can pour and measure safely.

Teacher key · Observe the action, not just the answer

Prepare two visibly different containers, then two manageable containers for direct comparison. Select small containers whose capacities can be measured safely with available tools. For improvised units, choose capacities that are exact cupfuls for an introductory trial; a later remainder must be recorded honestly. Use a tray and wipe spills immediately. Never use chemical or medicine containers.

  1. Capacity is the maximum amount a container can hold. Adding water changes its current contents, not its capacity.
  2. Accept the larger-capacity prediction with an observation for the deliberately obvious pair; check it physically. Height alone is not a general capacity rule.
  3. Starting with A full and B empty: space left in B means A has less capacity; exactly full means equal; overflow from full B means A has more. Spillage during transfer invalidates the trial.
  4. Accept any suitable unit choice with a reason; a cup is usually more convenient than a tiny spoon for a jug. Same-sized, equally filled units are essential; do not compare counts from different cups. Record incomplete final units separately.
  5. The jug did not change. Different units give different counts. Ten 100 mL portions total 1000 mL = 1 L. Recognise L/l and mL/ml; prefer capital L for clarity.
  6. Observe selection, levelling, eye position, pouring, reading and recording by each child. Capacity requires measuring full contents. Repeated portions must all be accounted for. Actual results depend on equipment; there is no invented answer.
  7. Estimate only after measurement experience; record it before measuring C. Assess use of a known reference and explanation of discrepancy, not a fixed percentage tolerance.
  8. 250 mL and 5 L. 1 L 30 mL = 1000 + 30 = 1030 mL; 2 L 250 mL = 2000 + 250 = 2250 mL; 3 L 5 mL = 3000 + 5 = 3005 mL. Do not require reverse conversion or decimal notation.

小三 · 容器能盛多少？

姓名 ______ 日期 ______ · 準備清潔、不易破碎的容器、托盤、水及毛巾，由成人指導。不可飲用活動用水，不用藥物、清潔劑、熱水或玻璃。

1 · 容量與現在的水量

指出杯子和水桶，解釋容量的意思。在杯中先放少量水，再加水但不溢出。杯子的容量改變了，還是杯中的水量改變了？畫圖或解釋。

2 · 觀察明顯的差別

觀察小杯和大得多的水壺，先不倒水。預測哪個盛滿時能盛較多水，說明觀察到甚麼，保留預測以便實測後比較。

3 · 直接比較

選兩個小容器A和B，約定盛滿至杯口，在托盤上操作。把A盛滿，再把全部水小心倒入空的B。記錄B仍有空位、剛好盛滿或水溢出，這說明兩個容量有甚麼關係？若B未滿時水已灑到外面，須重做，不可當作容量證據。交換角色，讓每位同學倒水及解釋。

4 · 選擇自訂單位

量水壺時，可選小杯或很小的匙作單位。哪個在這裏較合適？選擇並解釋原因。兩個容器都用同一個所選單位、每次盛至相同水位。數出盛滿A和B各需多少個完整單位；最後不足一個單位須另作記錄，不可當完整一個。每次只比較兩個容器。哪個容量較大？所選單位每次的份量相同，如何使比較公平？

5 · 為何要用標準單位？

兩組量同一個水壺，一組記錄4大杯，另一組記錄8小杯。水壺容量改變了嗎？為何不能只比較數字？建議一個共同單位。

觀察有1 L標記的容器及100 mL量杯。在指導下把量杯盛至100 mL刻度，倒入1 L容器，重複共十次。總量是多少？把升配對L或l，把毫升配對mL或ml。解釋為何大寫L較易與數字1分辨。

量度、估計及記錄

6 · 選用量度工具

先為小瓶選擇量程合適、細分格清晰的量杯，再為較大的水壺選工具。說明沒有刻度的杯或量程太小的工具為何較不方便。在托盤上把目標容器盛滿至口，再把全部水倒入量度工具，不可漏失。如需分次量度，逐次記錄再相加。

量杯平放在平坦表面，眼睛接近水面高度，讀取水面刻度而非容器頂端。記下單位和最小分格。量兩個容器並比較，分清盛滿的容量與未滿時的水量。

8 · 選合適單位記錄

選L或mL並解釋：小飲品盒標示250 ___；大水容器標示5 ___。已量得一瓶容量是1 L 30 mL，用mL記錄並解釋加法。再處理2 L 250 mL及3 L 5 mL，不用小數。閱讀安全、清潔的飲品容器標籤，抄下數字和單位；分清標示的內容物份量和容器最大容量。

再做已有的量杯小測。解釋為何屏幕讀數正確，並不證明能安全倒水和量度。

教師解答 · 觀察操作，不只看答案

先準備容量差別明顯的一對容器，再用兩個容易操作的小容器直接比較。初次自訂單位活動可選剛好整杯的容量，其後若有餘量必須如實記錄。使用托盤，立即抹乾灑水；不用化學品或藥物容器。

  1. 容量是容器最多能盛的量。加水改變當前水量，不改變容量。
  2. 就刻意選定的明顯差別，接受有觀察理由的預測，再實測；不能把高度當作一般容量規則。
  3. A滿、B空時，倒完後B有空位表示A容量較小；剛好滿表示相等；B已滿而溢水表示A較大。轉移時灑漏須重做。
  4. 接受任何合適的單位及理由；量水壺時，小杯通常比很小的匙方便。單位大小和每次水位須相同；不同杯的數量不能直接比較。末次不足一杯另記。
  5. 水壺容量不變，不同單位造成不同數量。十份100 mL合共1000 mL＝1 L。辨認L／l及mL／ml；大寫L較清楚。
  6. 逐人觀察選工具、放平、眼睛位置、倒水、讀數及記錄。量容量須盛滿，分次量度須計入全部份量。答案依實際工具而定，不預設讀數。
  7. 量度經驗後才估計，先記下估計才量C。評估參考量及誤差解釋，不設定武斷的百分比合格線。
  8. 250 mL及5 L。1 L 30 mL＝1000＋30＝1030 mL；2 L 250 mL＝2000＋250＝2250 mL；3 L 5 mL＝3000＋5＝3005 mL。不要求由單名數反轉為複名數或用小數。
