P6 · Pie charts

1 · Establish the whole

A supplied poll has 40 votes split 10, 20 and 10. Represent the shares with a paper circle folded into quarters; identify the whole before reading each part.

P6 · Pie charts

2 · Read percentages

For a labelled example chart with A 25%, B 50%, C 25%, and total 80, find each count. Verify that both percentages and counts sum to their whole.

P6 · Pie charts

3 · Read labelled angles

Use labelled sectors 90°, 135° and 135°, total 48. Find counts from the labels without measuring angles.

P6 · Pie charts

4 · Compare two wholes

Two charts show walking shares 20% and 25%. First hide the totals and explain what is unknown; then use totals 100 and 40 and compare the actual counts.

P6 · Pie charts

5 · Optional digital construction

Use a teacher-approved spreadsheet or drawing tool to produce a chart for the supplied 10, 20, 10 votes. Check labels and whole; hand-drawn sector construction is not required core work.

P6 · Pie charts

6 · Explain a limitation

Give one question a pie chart can answer and one that needs the total. Write a supported conclusion without inventing missing data.

Teacher models and observation record

Task 2 counts: 20, 40, 20. Task 3 counts: 12, 18, 18. Task 4 actual walking counts: 20 and 10, so the larger percentage does not give the larger count.
